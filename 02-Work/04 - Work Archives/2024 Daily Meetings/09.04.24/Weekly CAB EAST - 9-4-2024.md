A Kirk key cannot be used as an isolation device per NFPA 70E because it does not meet the requirements for ensuring an electrically safe work condition. Here are the main reasons:
 
1. **Verification of Absence of Voltage**: NFPA 70E requires the use of an adequately rated portable test instrument to verify the absence of voltage before any work can be performed on electrical equipment1. A Kirk key interlock system does not provide this verification.
2. **Physical Disconnection**: NFPA 70E mandates that an isolation device must physically disconnect the electrical equipment from all energy sources1. A Kirk key interlock system controls access and operation but does not physically disconnect the equipment.
3. **Lockout/Tagout Compliance**: The standard specifies that lockout/tagout procedures must be followed to ensure that equipment cannot be re-energized while maintenance is being performed1. A Kirk key system alone does not fulfill these lockout/tagout requirements.
