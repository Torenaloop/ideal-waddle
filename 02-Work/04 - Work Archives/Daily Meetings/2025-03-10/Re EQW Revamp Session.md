**Electrical Safety Workshop**

- Topic 2 - Review Electrical Safety Program: We will review the elements (Electrical Safety Program 110.3, pg. 16-17, and Training Requirements 10.4, pg. 17-19) of the NFPA 70E to show how they align with the STACK Infrastructure Electrical Safety Policy requirements. 

- Topic 2 - Job Safety Analysis: We will review a completed, approved JSA for an electrical task so that you can see what should be on the JSA. You will then be split up into equal groups to develop your own JSA for an electrical task. Each group will write a JSA for the same electrical task and we’ll do a comparison to see how they may differ. Keep in mind the hierarchy of controls that we just reviewed and shown in order – elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, PPE.

- Topic 5 – Energized Electrical Work Permit Review: STACK Infrastructure has a process in place for conducting work on energized conductors and circuit parts, and although every effort should be made to conduct work in a de-energized state, it may be necessary to work on equipment that is energized. This work would require an EEWP. I think we should adjust this and have a example EEWP filled out to review, and then the activity is to have the class split into 2 – 3 groups and fill out out, and then present, similar to the JSA activity.

- Topic 6 – Inspecting Rubber Insulating Gloves: Rubber insulating gloves act as PPE to protect the worker from shock due to inadvertent contact with energized conductors or circuit parts. Damage or defects to the rubber insulating gloves may not protect the worker as intended, which could lead to serious injury or death, and must be inspected prior to use. I think we need to include inspecting arc flash suits also.

- Not a current Activity: I think we need to have an activity of how to Don and Doff PPE and how to properly hold and maneuver a rescue hook.

   
**Control of Hazardous Energies Workshop**

- Topic 2 - Review Energy Control Program: We will review the main elements of each document to demonstrate the alignment with the STACK Infrastructure Energy Control Program and OSHA requirements. 

- Topic 3 – LOCKOUT/TAGOUT Device Review: There are many types of lockout/tagout devices available, and we'll have a look at what can be found in the site lockout stations and what you might have in stock. We'll also review how the different devices operate and their specific application. I think this is where the LOTO trainer can be useful.

- Topic 5 – LOCKOUT/TAGOUT Procedure Review: Writing equipment specific procedures requires knowledge of the operation of that equipment, and the hazards that must be controlled for that equipment. Detailed lockout/tagout procedures should tell you what the energy is (electrical, mechanical, gravity, rotating equipment, etc.), level of energy under operating conditions (voltage, amperage, PSI), where that energy is located (room # or physical location such as the roof or generator yard), energy isolating device identifier (valve, disconnect, breaker or switch number/name). These detailed procedures inform the worker of the hazards associated with the equipment and the required steps to isolate that energy to prevent injuries, fatalities, or interruptions to uptime. 

- Topic 5 – Portable Test Meter Selection: Portable test equipment comes with ratings from the manufacturer regarding the maximum voltage the test equipment can be exposed to and the category of electrical equipment or service the portable test equipment is rated for. I think if the LOTO test kit has power, we can also do a LDL utilizing the test kit.
