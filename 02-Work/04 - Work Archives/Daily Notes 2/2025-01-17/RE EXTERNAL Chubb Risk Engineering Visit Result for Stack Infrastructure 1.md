Hi Donna,  
   
Here is my feedback on their recommendations:  
   
**Fire Response Planning for Lithium-Ion Systems**  
I do not agree with their recommendation regarding our Lithium batteries. Just a guess but they likely did not look into the types of lithium-ion before making this recommendation. Most people see Tesla battery fires, cellphone batteries exploding on airplanes, and various other news about the dangers of them. However, the batteries we have are Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4). With a standard Lithium-Ion battery fire can propagate from one jar to another easily. The same can be said for VRLA batteries during a thermal runaway event. Lithium Iron Phosphate batteries do not have this problem. Narada tested this design in accordance with ANSI/CAN/UL 9540A. Multiple samples were tested, and cells were forced into a thermal runaway event. The fire never propagated to another jar and was contained to the single failure. Those results mean these are safer than VRLAs.  
   
A couple more things to note:

- Lithium-Ion batteries do not off gas. VRLAs can off gas during certain conditions and they release hydrogen, which is obviously dangerous. Lithium batteries do not do this.
- When these were installed our sprinkler company brought out their engineer to verify we had sufficient fire suppression in place, we do.
- NFPA suggests ABC extinguishers because it could be either a class B or C fire. We have all ABCs in the facility.
- The city inspected our installation and site for the permit. All firewalls and fire doors are installed according to code and the city’s requirements.

   
**Water Damage Mitigation Program**  
Do we really need it? I don’t think so. All our equipment is installed on 4” concrete pads. We would need a ridiculous amount of water to be released for it to become a problem. Additionally, in the areas where it really matters, we have the pre-action fire system. Accidental release in those areas would be unlikely. The only release that could impact us would be if a 10” chilled water pipe burst. The pressure is so low that it wouldn’t be an instant flood but all the water in the pipe could be released if the hole was big enough. In that scenario, our equipment being up on 4” pads would protect us.  
   
**Inspection of the Lightning Arrestor System**  
I agree we should be inspecting this, and as Tom pointed out, it is called for in the maintenance standard. This has been something we have discussed with TJ and Spencer many times and a real solution/decision has never been made. The problem is we don’t have a safe way to do it. We inspect the system on the main roof, that’s not a problem. The chilled water storage towers, which are the highest point, are where the issue comes in. We don’t have a safe way to get up there. Our 2-man lift is not high enough and according to David N the ladders that are installed on the towers can’t be used. Since we would never need a reason to get up there besides this inspection it got put on the back burner. I am open to any suggestions though.
