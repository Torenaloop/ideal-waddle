**6. Be Ready for Emergencies:**

- Ensure vehicles are winter-ready with properly inflated tires, a full gas tank, and functioning wipers and defrosters.
- Keep an emergency kit in your vehicle, including blankets, water, a flashlight, and extra warm clothing.
- Drive cautiously and maintain a safe following distance, especially on icy or snow-covered roads.
- Avoid sudden braking or sharp turns to prevent skidding.
- Clear snow and ice from all windows, mirrors, and lights before driving.
- Stay updated on road conditions and plan routes accordingly.

**5. Driving Safety:**

- Do not work alone.
- Watch for symptoms of hypothermia or frostbite in yourself and coworkers.
- Immediately report unsafe conditions, such as icy surfaces or exposed hazards, to your supervisor.
- Limit exposed skin and wear protective gear.
- Postpone higher-risk tasks when possible.

**4. Prioritize Health and Safety:**

- Drain all water lines where possible to prevent freezing.
- Inspect machinery and tools to ensure they function correctly in low temperatures.
- Apply de-icing agents to walkways and access points to reduce slip hazards. Clear walkways before any snow or ice accumulation.
- Secure materials and structures against high winds and ice buildup.
- Store fluids and equipment with fluids indoors when safe to do so.
- Warm up vehicles and equipment before use.
- Be aware that cold weather can make materials more brittle—handle with care.

**3. Prepare Equipment and the Site:**

- Cold weather can still dehydrate you—drink water regularly.
- Take scheduled warm-up breaks in designated heated areas.
- Check in with your teammates to ensure everyone is doing okay.

**2. Stay Hydrated and Take Breaks:**

- Layer up with insulated clothing to stay warm.
- Wear gloves, hats, and thermal socks to prevent frostbite.
- Use insulated, waterproof boots with good traction to prevent slips.
- Minimize time working outdoors.
