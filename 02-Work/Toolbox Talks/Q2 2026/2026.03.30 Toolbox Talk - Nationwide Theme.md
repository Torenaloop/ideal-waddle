


**TOOLBOX TALK – DISTRACTED DRIVING AWARENESS (APRIL)**

Distracted Driving Awareness Month takes place each April and highlights a risk many of us face every day—on the road before and after work. Whether commuting to a data center, traveling between sites, or running errands, even a brief distraction can have serious consequences. In fast-paced environments like ours, staying focused doesn’t stop at the job site—it extends to how we get there and back. Taking a few simple steps can prevent incidents that impact not just you, but your team and others on the road.

**What Creates Risk?**  
Distracted driving is anything that takes your attention away from the road. The most common risks include:

- **Mobile phone use:** Texting, checking emails, or using apps while driving
    
- **In-vehicle distractions:** Adjusting navigation, music, or climate controls
    
- **Mental distractions:** Thinking about work tasks, fatigue, or stress
    
- **Eating or drinking while driving**
    
- **Fatigue-related inattention**, especially during early or late shifts
    

Even a few seconds of distraction at highway speeds can mean traveling the length of a football field without looking at the road.

**Practical Ways to Stay Focused**

- **Set up before you go:** Enter directions, adjust controls, and secure items before driving
    
- **Put the phone away:** Use “Do Not Disturb” or hands-free only when absolutely necessary
    
- **Stay alert:** If you’re tired after a long shift, take a break before driving
    
- **Avoid multitasking:** Driving deserves your full attention—save meals, messages, and adjustments for later
    
- **Plan your commute:** Give yourself enough time so you don’t feel rushed or tempted to cut corners
    
- **Watch for others:** Defensive driving is key—assume others may be distracted
    

Driving is one of the most routine yet highest-risk activities we do each day. Staying focused behind the wheel helps ensure you arrive safely—every time.
