The MOP identifies the physical work as PPE donning, vacuum excavation to locate sanitary/waterline tie-in points and utility crossings, mechanical excavation, trench box installation, sanitary and waterline live taps, pipe installation, backfill while moving the trench box, and cleanup. It also identifies six utility crossings, trench depth, and a 40 psi utility-water pressure exposure. The submitted JSA currently covers only three task rows: locating existing utilities, trench excavation, and utility crossings.

**Rationale:** Controls were matched to the closest standard hazard themes for excavation/trenching, utility strikes, struck-by/caught-between exposure, pressurized-line work, sanitary/biological exposure, material handling, backfill, and housekeeping based on the MOP’s physical work scope.

## Gap Analysis and Recommendations

- ✅ **Complete/Aligned:** The JSA correctly identifies utility-location work, utility-damage risk, trench excavation, unstable soils, and the need for vacuum excavation and trench box protection. These align with the MOP’s listed utility crossings, trench depth concern, vacuum excavation requirement, and trench protection requirement.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA does not include the live waterline tap or sanitary live tap. Add hazards for pressurized water release, flooding, cutting/drilling exposure, biological/sanitary exposure, splash exposure, and sharp pipe/cutting hazards.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA does not address mechanical excavation hazards from the trackhoe and loader, including struck-by, caught-between, swing-radius exposure, blind spots, equipment operating near trench edges, and utility strike during excavation.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA does not include pipe installation inside the trench box. Add controls for protected-zone work, mechanical assistance/team lifting, pinch-point control, communication with equipment operators, and keeping personnel clear of suspended loads.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA does not include backfill or trench box movement. Add controls requiring personnel to remain clear during trench box movement/backfill, spotter use, controlled backfill placement, and competent-person reinspection before re-entry.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA required PPE list is missing safety glasses/face shield and hearing protection, both of which are listed in the MOP’s minimum PPE.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA’s “Required Training” field appears blank. For this scope, consider listing excavation/trenching competent person, qualified equipment operator, site/vendor safety orientation, utility-locate awareness, and task-specific live tap procedure training.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing or Incomplete:** The JSA uses “damage to the utility” as the primary utility hazard, but it should also address personnel injury and operational impacts from electrical utility strike, pressurized water release, flooding, sanitary exposure, and disruption to facility utilities.
    
- 💡 **Recommendation:** Replace generic controls such as “Vac Excavation” with action-oriented controls such as: “Confirm utility markings, vacuum excavate/pothole crossings before mechanical excavation, maintain spotter communication, and stop work if unmarked utilities are encountered.”
    
- 💡 **Recommendation:** Replace “Trench box installation” with more complete controls: “Competent person shall inspect trench conditions before entry, install rated trench protection before personnel enter, keep spoils/equipment back from trench edges, provide safe access/egress, and reinspect after weather, vibration, or trench box movement.”
    
- 💡 **Recommendation:** Correct the task sequence in the JSA so utility crossings are located/verified before or during controlled excavation, not after trench excavation. The MOP indicates vacuum excavation of tie-in locations and utility crossings occurs before mechanical excavation.
    
- ✅ **Hazards Not Relevant to MOP:** No clearly irrelevant hazards were identified in the submitted JSA. The issue is primarily that the JSA is underdeveloped compared with the procedure scope.
    

**Clarification needed:** The JSA lists “Utility crossings” after “Trench Excavation,” while the MOP indicates utility crossings are vacuum-excavated before mechanical excavation. Confirm whether the JSA sequence should be revised to show utility locating/crossing verification before trench excavation begins.
