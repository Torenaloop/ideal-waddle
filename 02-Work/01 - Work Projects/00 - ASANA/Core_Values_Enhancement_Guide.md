## What Partnership Looks Like

### In Leadership
**Partnership-Focused Leaders:**
- Seek input before making decisions
- Build relationships across organizational boundaries
- Share information openly and proactively
- Give credit generously
- View success as collective, not individual
- Remove barriers to collaboration
- Model collaborative behaviors

- "Let me check with the Operations team first"
- "What perspective am I missing?"
- "How can we work on this together?"
- "That success belongs to the whole team"

### In Teams
**Partnership-Focused Teams:**
- Reach out to other teams proactively
- Share knowledge and best practices
- Support other teams' initiatives
- Celebrate cross-functional wins
- View other teams as partners, not competitors
- Communicate regularly across boundaries

**Example Behaviors:**
- Regular cross-team meetings
- Shared project ownership
- Knowledge sharing sessions
- Joint problem-solving
- Mutual support and backup

### In Individuals
**Partnership-Focused Individuals:**
- Build relationships across organizational boundaries
- Seek to understand others' perspectives
- Offer help without being asked
- Share information that helps others
- Give credit to partners
- View collaboration as strength, not weakness

**Example Behaviors:**
- "I know someone who can help with that"
- "Let me introduce you to..."
- "Here's something that might help your team"
- "We figured this out together"

**Barrier:** Teams focus only on their own goals  
**Solution:** Align on shared objectives, recognize collective wins

#### 5. "Not My Job" Mentality
**Barrier:** People stay within their defined roles  
**Solution:** Reward collaboration, celebrate partnership behaviors

## What Visible Looks Like

### In Leadership
**Visible Leaders:**
- **Are present** - Regularly in the field, on sites, with teams
- **Are accessible** - Easy to reach, responsive to questions
- **Listen actively** - Give full attention when with people
- **Show authenticity** - Genuine interest, not performative
- **Follow through** - Remember conversations, take action on input
- **Create space** - Make time for informal interactions
- **Model values** - Demonstrate what's important through presence

**Example Behaviors:**
- Regular site visits
- Walking around, not just formal meetings
- Open-door policies (and actually being there)
- Attending team events
- Responding to messages promptly
- Remembering names and previous conversations

### In Teams
**Visible Teams:**
- **Show up** - Present at meetings, events, cross-functional gatherings
- **Engage actively** - Participate, don't just attend
- **Share openly** - Communicate status, challenges, wins
- **Support visibly** - Show up for other teams' initiatives
- **Celebrate together** - Be present for wins and milestones

**Example Behaviors:**
- Attending cross-functional meetings
- Participating in organizational events
- Sharing updates proactively
- Supporting other teams publicly

### In Individuals
**Visible Individuals:**
- **Show up fully** - Present and engaged, not distracted
- **Communicate proactively** - Share status without being asked
- **Make themselves available** - Accessible for questions and help
- **Participate actively** - Contribute in meetings and discussions
- **Follow through visibly** - Others see actions matching words

**Example Behaviors:**
- Phone away during conversations
- Regular updates on work progress
- Offering to help visibly
- Speaking up in meetings
- Completing commitments publicly

## Visibility in Practice

### Leadership Site Visits
**The Practice:** Regular, meaningful time on sites and in field

**What Makes It Work:**
- **Frequency** - Regular, not just when problems arise
- **Authenticity** - Genuine interest, not inspection
- **Listening** - More questions than statements
- **Follow-through** - Act on what you learn
- **Consistency** - Predictable presence

**What to Avoid:**
- Drive-by visits with no real engagement
- Only showing up for problems
- Talking more than listening
- Making promises without follow-through
- Inconsistent or unpredictable presence

### Accessible Leadership
**The Practice:** Leaders are easy to reach and responsive

**What Makes It Work:**
- **Multiple channels** - Phone, email, in-person, all accessible
- **Responsiveness** - Timely replies to questions
- **Open-door reality** - Policy matches practice
- **Informal opportunities** - Coffee, walking meetings, casual conversations
- **Two-way communication** - Leaders seek input, not just broadcast

### Town Halls & Team Meetings
**The Practice:** Regular forums for connection and communication

**What Makes It Work:**
- **Regular cadence** - Consistent schedule
- **Two-way dialogue** - Q&A, not just presentations
- **Authenticity** - Honest, not just positive spin
- **Action** - Visible follow-through from previous meetings
- **Inclusivity** - All levels invited and encouraged to participate

---

## Barriers to Visibility (And How to Overcome Them)

### Common Barriers

#### 1. "Too Busy"
**Barrier:** Leaders claim no time for visibility  
**Solution:** Visibility is the work, not separate from it

**Reframe:**
- Site visits = understanding real conditions
- Team conversations = gathering critical intelligence
- Presence = building trust that enables performance

#### 2. Digital-Only Leadership
**Barrier:** All communication is email/messages  
**Solution:** Balance digital with face-to-face

**Actions:**
- Schedule regular in-person time
- Video on for virtual meetings
- Pick up phone for important conversations
- Visit sites and teams regularly

#### 3. Office-Bound
**Barrier:** Leaders stay in offices/headquarters  
**Solution:** Get out to where the work happens

**Actions:**
- Regular site visit schedule
- Work from different locations
- Attend field team meetings
- Eat lunch with different groups

#### 4. Inauthenticity
**Barrier:** Presence feels performative, not genuine  
**Solution:** Show real interest and follow through

**Actions:**
- Listen more than talk
- Remember and reference previous conversations
- Take action on input received
- Be honest about challenges

## Barriers to Culture Carrying (And How to Overcome Them)

### Common Barriers

#### 1. "Culture is HR's Job"
**Barrier:** Viewing culture as someone else's responsibility  
**Solution:** Culture is everyone's responsibility

**Reframe:**
- Every interaction shapes culture
- Every person either strengthens or weakens culture
- Culture carriers at all levels, not just HR or leadership

#### 2. Inconsistency
**Barrier:** Values are stated but not lived  
**Solution:** Model values consistently, especially when hard

**Actions:**
- Make values-based decisions even at cost
- Address misalignment promptly
- Recognize and reward cultural alignment
- Leadership models values visibly

#### 3. Lack of Clarity
**Barrier:** People don't understand what culture means  
**Solution:** Make culture concrete and visible

**Actions:**
- Define values clearly with examples
- Share culture stories regularly
- Provide specific behavioral expectations
- Give feedback on cultural alignment

#### 4. External Partnerships Ignored
**Barrier:** Culture only matters inside organization  
**Solution:** Extend culture to all partnerships

**Actions:**
- Include culture in vendor selection
- Onboard vendors to values
- Measure and manage vendor cultural alignment
- Recognize vendors who embody values
