The **refrigeration cycle** is the process used by refrigerators, air conditioners, and heat pumps to transfer heat from one location to another, typically from a cooler area to a warmer area, using a refrigerant. This cycle relies on principles of thermodynamics, specifically the phase changes of a refrigerant, to absorb and release heat.  
Here’s a breakdown of the steps in a typical **vapor-compression refrigeration cycle**, which is the most common type:
 
**1. Compression**

- **What Happens**:
    - The refrigerant, in a low-pressure gas state, enters the **compressor**.
    - The compressor increases the pressure and temperature of the refrigerant gas.
- **Key Purpose**:
    - Compressing the refrigerant raises its temperature above the ambient temperature, allowing heat to be released in the next step.
- **Location**: Typically found on the back or bottom of a refrigerator or in the outdoor unit of an air conditioner.
 
**2. Condensation**

- **What Happens**:
    - The high-pressure, high-temperature refrigerant gas flows into the **condenser**, usually a coil of metal tubing with fins to increase surface area.
    - The refrigerant releases heat to the surrounding environment (e.g., air or water) and condenses into a high-pressure liquid.
- **Key Purpose**:
    - To expel the heat absorbed by the refrigerant inside the system.
- **Location**: Found outside the cooled space, often on the back of the appliance or in an external unit.
 
**3. Expansion (Throttling)**

- **What Happens**:
    - The high-pressure liquid refrigerant passes through an **expansion valve** (or capillary tube), which lowers its pressure rapidly.
    - As the pressure drops, the refrigerant’s temperature decreases significantly.
- **Key Purpose**:
    - To prepare the refrigerant for the next step, where it absorbs heat.
- **Location**: Found between the condenser and the evaporator.
 
**4. Evaporation**

- **What Happens**:
    - The low-pressure, low-temperature refrigerant enters the **evaporator** coil, usually located inside the cooled space (e.g., refrigerator or air handler).
    - The refrigerant absorbs heat from the surrounding air, causing it to evaporate into a low-pressure gas.
- **Key Purpose**:
    - To absorb heat from the space that needs to be cooled.
- **Location**: Found inside the appliance or cooled space.
 
**Cycle Repeats**  
After the refrigerant has absorbed heat and evaporated, it flows back to the compressor to restart the cycle.
 
**Key Components**

1. **Compressor**: Increases refrigerant pressure and temperature.
2. **Condenser**: Releases heat to the surroundings.
3. **Expansion Valve**: Reduces refrigerant pressure and temperature.
4. **Evaporator**: Absorbs heat from the cooled space.
 
**Energy Transfer**  
The cycle moves heat:

- From the inside of a space (e.g., the refrigerated compartment) where the evaporator absorbs it.
- To the outside environment, where the condenser releases it.

This heat transfer is powered by the compressor, which requires external energy (usually electricity) to function.
 
**Applications**

1. **Refrigerators**: Maintain a cool interior by expelling heat to the room.
2. **Air Conditioners**: Cool indoor spaces by transferring heat to the outside.
3. **Heat Pumps**: Work in reverse during heating mode to move heat into a space.
