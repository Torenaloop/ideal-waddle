**On the War of the Roses tradition** — worth taking seriously, with one specific concern.

Traditions of that kind are common and rarely verifiable, for a structural reason: to be documented as aligned with a principal between 1455 and 1487, a family generally had to be armigerous. Records of ordinary combatants essentially don't exist. Muster rolls, attainders and heraldic visitations cover the gentry and above.

The specific concern is this. The Geni chain already contains **Joan Howard**. The Howards were Yorkist principals — John Howard, 1st Duke of Norfolk, died at Bosworth in 1485 fighting for Richard III. And the Geni generations alive during the Wars are George Nicholls (b. 1431) and John Nicholls (c. 1450), which is precisely the stretch of unsourced, place-less D-grade material.

So there's a real possibility your family tradition and the Geni graft are the same story in different clothes — either the tradition fed the tree, or a relative encountered the tree and the tradition grew from it.

That's testable, and it's the first thing I'd do. **Work the tradition's provenance before its content:**

- Who told you, and who told them? How many links back does the telling go?
- Does it name a specific principal, or just "the War of the Roses"? If it names **Howard**, that's a strong sign of contamination from the online trees. If it names someone unconnected — Percy, Neville, Stafford, Beauchamp — that's independent and considerably more interesting.
- Is there a date on the earliest version? A tradition attested in a 1930s letter is a different animal from one that surfaced after 2005.
