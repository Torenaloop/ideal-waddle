| Cripple Creek Confluence Area (Charles Nuckolls' 1,000 acres) |      | Ivanhoe, VA — Cripple Creek flows into the New River here, between Austinville and Ivanhoe. B.F. Nuckolls records Charles entering 1,000 acres 'on New River and Cripple Creek' in 1780, when this was still Montgomery County. Charles served as a Justice at Grayson's first court in 1793, confirming his residence just inside the new county line. |         | [Open](https://www.google.com/maps/place/?q=place_id:ChIJ1dNGb1vlUYgRVgkFoj0oUec) |
| New River Trail — Austinville Trailhead                       |      | The Wythe/Grayson county boundary ran near here. Austinville was the site of John Chiswell's lead mines (1756), which drew early settlers including the Nuckolls family to this river corridor. The New River Trail now follows the exact route of the old Cripple Creek Extension railroad.                                                            |         | [Open](https://www.google.com/maps/place/?q=place_id:ChIJufQ5eYHxUYgRBrdyS_YPBmo) |
| Fries, Virginia (northern Grayson County)                     |      | A mill town on the New River within what was Grayson County. The New River flows north through this corridor before reaching the Cripple Creek confluence at Ivanhoe. Charles' 1,000-acre tract likely ran along this river bottomland.                                                                                                                 |         | [Open](https://www.google.com/maps/place/?q=place_id:ChIJmds7DazuUYgR7bnJwIfyfL8) |
| Old Town / Greenville (Grayson Court House)                   |      | The original county seat where the first Grayson County court was held in May 1793, at 'Rose's Cabbins.' Charles Nuckolls was named a Justice of the Court at this first session.
