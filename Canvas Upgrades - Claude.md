Here's a suggested spatial layout for your canvas, organized into functional zones with a clear information hierarchy. The key idea is a **priority gradient from top to bottom**: things you need to see first are at the top, reference material sinks to the bottom.

**Top — "This week" focal card** is the single most important addition. It's a small note you update at the start of each week (or daily) with your top 3 priorities. It anchors the whole canvas and gives you an instant answer to "what should I be doing right now?" Link it to your current daily note.

**Middle left — Task management** keeps your Asana embed (sized large, since it's your primary task system) stacked above the Obsidian-native open tasks list. Everything actionable lives here.

**Middle right — Recurring meetings** collects all your MOC cards in a tight grid. They're small because they're jump-off points, not destinations — you click through to the actual meeting note.

**Bottom left — People & cadence** groups your direct report cards, the site visit rotation tracker, and topics for leadership. This is your "people management" corner.

**Bottom right — Reference & ideas** is where the 4Ds framework, lessons learned, and AI collab notes live. Valuable but not urgent — you consult these, not act on them moment-to-moment.

In practice, you'd use Canvas groups (right-click → "Add group") to create the dashed boundary zones, then drag your existing cards into position. Want to start implementing this, or tweak the zone arrangement first?
