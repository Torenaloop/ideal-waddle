The velocity of a baseball pitch comes from a combination of biomechanical factors, muscular strength, and efficient energy transfer through the kinetic chain. Here's a breakdown of where the velocity originates during a pitch:
 
**1. Kinetic Chain**  
The kinetic chain refers to the sequential movement of body segments that transfer energy from the ground to the baseball. Each segment builds upon the previous one to generate maximum velocity:

- **Lower Body**:
    - The legs push off the ground to create force (ground reaction force).
    - The stride generates momentum toward the target.
- **Hips and Core**:
    - The rotation of the hips and core transfers energy from the lower body to the upper body.
- **Upper Body**:
    - The torso rotates and leans forward, adding power to the throwing motion.
- **Shoulder and Arm**:
    - The shoulder externally rotates during the cocking phase and then rapidly internally rotates during the release.
- **Wrist and Fingers**:
    - The final energy transfer occurs as the wrist snaps forward and the fingers impart spin and release the ball.
 
**2. Ground Reaction Force**  
The pitcher’s forceful push off the mound with their back leg (also called the "drive leg") provides the initial energy. This energy is transmitted through the body in an upward and forward direction.
 
**3. Stride and Momentum**  
The stride length and direction contribute to velocity by:

- Covering ground toward the plate, adding forward momentum.
- Creating a stable base for the energy transfer to continue through the upper body.
 
**4. Hip and Shoulder Separation**  
The separation between the rotation of the hips and the rotation of the shoulders (called **torque**) is a critical factor:

- The hips rotate first, creating a delay before the shoulders rotate.
- This separation allows the upper body to "whip" forward, increasing velocity.
 
**5. Arm Speed and Shoulder Rotation**  
The arm acts as a lever:

- The rotation of the shoulder (internal rotation) and the extension of the elbow contribute significantly to the speed of the ball.
- Proper arm mechanics reduce strain and maximize velocity.
 
**6. Wrist and Finger Action**  
The final flick of the wrist and release of the ball from the fingertips add spin and velocity. This motion is small but crucial in ensuring that energy from the kinetic chain is fully imparted to the baseball.
 
**7. Factors Influencing Velocity**  
Several factors can impact a pitcher's ability to generate velocity:

1. **Strength and Conditioning**:
    - A strong core, legs, and shoulder muscles provide the power necessary for the kinetic chain.
2. **Flexibility**:
    - Increased range of motion in the hips, shoulders, and arms allows for greater torque and whip action.
3. **Mechanics**:
    - Proper pitching mechanics ensure efficient energy transfer and reduce energy leaks.
4. **Body Mass and Size**:
    - Heavier or taller pitchers often generate more momentum, contributing to velocity.
5. **Neuromuscular Coordination**:
    - Efficient timing and coordination of body movements maximize power output.
 
**Summary**  
Pitch velocity comes from the efficient use of the entire body, starting with the legs and transferring energy through the hips, core, shoulder, arm, and finally the wrist and fingers. Proper mechanics and physical conditioning are essential to optimize this process and achieve high velocities while minimizing the risk of injury.
