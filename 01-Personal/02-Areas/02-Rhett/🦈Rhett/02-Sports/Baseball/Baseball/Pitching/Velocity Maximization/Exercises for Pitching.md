For a 10-year-old aspiring pitcher, exercises should focus on developing **core stability**, **leg strength**, and **shoulder stability** without overloading the growing body. The key is to emphasize proper technique, body weight exercises, and age-appropriate movements to build strength and coordination safely.  
Here’s a breakdown of effective exercises:
 
**Core Strengthening Exercises**  
A strong core helps transfer energy efficiently through the kinetic chain during pitching.

1. **Plank**
    - How: Hold a forearm plank position with a straight back for 20–30 seconds.
    - Progression: Gradually increase the duration.
    - Focus: Builds core stability and endurance.
2. **Bird Dog**
    - How: Start on hands and knees. Extend the right arm and left leg simultaneously, hold for 3 seconds, then switch sides.
    - Focus: Improves balance and core control.
3. **Dead Bug**
    - How: Lie on your back with arms and legs raised. Lower one arm and the opposite leg toward the floor, then return to starting position. Alternate sides.
    - Focus: Enhances coordination and core engagement.
4. **Russian Twists (Without Weight)**
    - How: Sit with knees bent and feet slightly off the ground. Twist the torso side to side, lightly tapping the ground.
    - Focus: Improves rotational strength needed for pitching.
 
**Leg Strengthening Exercises**  
Strong legs are the foundation for generating power during the pitching motion.

1. **Bodyweight Squats**
    - How: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart. Lower hips down and back like sitting in a chair, then return to standing.
    - Reps: 3 sets of 10–12.
    - Focus: Builds lower body strength and balance.
2. **Lunges**
    - How: Step forward with one leg, lower the back knee toward the ground, then return to standing. Alternate legs.
    - Reps: 3 sets of 8 per leg.
    - Focus: Strengthens quadriceps, hamstrings, and glutes.
3. **Calf Raises**
    - How: Stand on the edge of a step or flat surface. Rise onto toes, hold briefly, then lower back down.
    - Reps: 3 sets of 15.
    - Focus: Strengthens calves for stability and balance.
4. **Step-Ups**
    - How: Step onto a sturdy platform or bench with one leg, then step down. Alternate legs.
    - Focus: Improves single-leg strength and coordination.
 
**Shoulder Strengthening Exercises**  
Healthy and strong shoulders are essential for pitching while reducing the risk of injury.

1. **Wall Angels**
    - How: Stand with your back against a wall. Slowly raise and lower arms in a "snow angel" motion while keeping them in contact with the wall.
    - Focus: Improves shoulder mobility and stability.
2. **Towel Shoulder Rotations**
    - How: Hold a small towel or light object. Rotate the shoulder outward (external rotation) and inward (internal rotation) against light resistance.
    - Focus: Strengthens rotator cuff muscles.
3. **Arm Circles**
    - How: Extend arms to the sides and make small circles forward and backward.
    - Reps: 30 seconds each direction.
    - Focus: Builds endurance and warms up shoulder muscles.
4. **Band Pull-Aparts** (with light resistance band)
    - How: Hold a resistance band with both hands at shoulder height. Pull the band apart until the arms are fully extended, then return.
    - Focus: Strengthens shoulder blades and improves posture.
 
**Dynamic and Functional Exercises**  
These exercises mimic movements in pitching and improve overall athleticism.

1. **Medicine Ball Rotational Throws (Lightweight Ball)**
    - How: Stand sideways to a wall. Hold a light medicine ball, rotate the torso, and throw the ball against the wall. Catch and repeat.
    - Focus: Builds rotational power and core strength.
2. **Skipping**
    - How: Perform skipping drills to improve coordination, leg strength, and explosive power.
    - Focus: Develops leg drive and balance.
3. **Bounding**
    - How: Take exaggerated, powerful steps forward with an emphasis on height and distance.
    - Focus: Builds explosive leg strength for the push-off phase in pitching.
4. **Ladder Drills**
    - How: Use a speed ladder to practice quick foot movements, like side shuffles or forward hops.
    - Focus: Improves agility and coordination.
 
**Guidelines for a 10-Year-Old**

- **Frequency**: 2–3 times per week, with rest days in between.
- **Duration**: Keep workouts short (20–30 minutes) to match their attention span.
- **Focus on Fun**: Incorporate games or challenges to keep them engaged.
- **Proper Warm-Up**: Start with light cardio (jumping jacks, jogging) and dynamic stretches to prevent injuries.
- **Avoid Overtraining**: Limit intense throwing or resistance exercises to protect developing joints.
