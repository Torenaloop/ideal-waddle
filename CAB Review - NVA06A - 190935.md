## Gap Analysis and Recommendations

- ✅ **Complete/Aligned:** The JSA correctly recognizes electrical energy, arc-flash exposure, and the need for arc-flash controls during MSB breaker operation. AMS may be an appropriate control where the applicable breaker has an approved arc-reduction function.
    
- ⚠️ **Scope mismatch:** The JSA is for **Li-on Tamer testing**, while the MOP is for **SKRU solenoid replacement/remediation** under Work Order 190935. The activity title, scope, equipment identification, and approval basis should be revised to match this specific MOP.
    
- ⚠️ **Insufficient task coverage:** The JSA’s single breaker-operation row does not cover HMI transfers, SKRU key exchanges, LOTO, battery isolation, live-dead-live testing, vendor solenoid work, re-energization, converter startup, functional transfer testing, or final physical cleanup.
    
- ⚠️ **Missing hazards:** Electrical shock, stored DC battery energy, spring-charged breaker energy, pinch and line-of-fire exposure, incorrect sequencing, unexpected energization, failed test instrumentation, vendor-interface exposure, and critical-load interruption are absent.
    
- ⚠️ **Incomplete controls:** “Wear arc flash gear” and “utilize AMS” are too general. The JSA should identify where AMS applies, require positive equipment identification, two-person verification, load-sharing checks, personal LOTO, absence-of-voltage verification, test-instrument validation, stop-work criteria, and lock-owner removal.
    
- ⚠️ **PPE conflict:** The MOP lists incident-energy values as high as **59.2 cal/cm²**, but its detailed PPE tables list cut-resistant gloves, hard hat, safety glasses/goggles, hearing protection, and leather work shoes without defining the arc-rated ensemble or shock-protection gloves. The JSA’s phrase “arc flash gear” also does not establish the equipment-specific PPE requirement. PPE and energized-work methods must be reconciled with each equipment label and the site electrical safe-work program before execution.
    
- ⚠️ **Potential procedure conflict:** MOP step 35 directs personnel to doff PPE immediately before the vendor’s live-dead-live test in step 36. The procedure does not clearly state that the vendor remains in the required arc-rated and shock-protection PPE for both live portions of the test.
    
- ⚠️ **LOTO restoration gap:** The MOP requires the vendor to apply LOTO at step 26, but the restoration section does not contain a clearly identified step for the vendor to remove its personal lock before the breakers are closed.
    
- ⚠️ **Sequence ambiguity:** The MOP commands an HMI transfer at UPS-R1-140/240-01 and then verifies both -01 and -02 in static bypass. It is not clear from the procedure whether the -01 HMI command intentionally controls both modules or whether a corresponding -02 transfer step is missing.
    
- ⚠️ **Equipment/PPE-basis ambiguity:** The incident-energy table does not clearly list matching values for every -01 and -02 MIB/MOB device operated later in the procedure, and one equipment identifier appears as “M0B” rather than “MOB.” Exact equipment IDs and label values should be verified before the JSA and PPE selection are approved.
    
- ✅ **Irrelevant hazard review:** Arc flash is relevant to the MOP. The irrelevant content is the JSA’s **Li-on Tamer testing scope**, not the arc-flash hazard itself.
    
- 💡 **Recommendation:** Issue a revised, task-specific JSA tied to this MOP and work order. Use the task rows above, incorporate the vendor’s solenoid-replacement JSA, define electrical PPE by the exact equipment labels, and resolve the procedure ambiguities before authorization.
