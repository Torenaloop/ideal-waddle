Let’s be real, cooking in a college dorm isn’t easy. Tiny communal kitchens, barely any counter space and maybe just a microwave to work with? We’ve been there. But that doesn’t mean you’re stuck with ramen and granola bars forever. Whether you’re craving something quick between classes or need a midnight snack, these dorm-friendly dishes have your back.

## 1\. Chicken Quesadillas

![An overhead image of a white plate with three triangles of chicken quesadilla. One of them is topped with sour cream, pico de gallo and guacamole. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Chicken-Quesadillas-lmjq/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Chicken-Quesadillas-lmjq-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Julia Gartland for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Samantha Seneviratne.

This satisfying snack or meal from Ali Slagle comes together in 10 minutes or less. The hack: Buy a Rotisserie chicken, or grab some grilled chicken from the dining hall. Shred the meat when it’s cool so that it doesn’t make the tortilla soggy. Cook the quesadilla over moderate heat, so the cheese melts just as the tortilla becomes crisp and golden.

Recipe: **[Chicken Quesadillas](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1025033-chicken-quesadillas)**

## 2\. Microwave Peanut Butter and Jelly Cake

![An overhead shot of a pink glass bowl with microwave peanut butter and jelly cake topped with a scoop of vanilla ice cream.](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/08/03/multimedia/03FD-DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-microwavepeanutbuttercake-tkcp/03FD-DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-microwavepeanutbuttercake-tkcp-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Andrew Bui for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Kaitlin Wayne.

Carolina Gelen’s microwave dessert delivers all the nostalgia of a PB&J in a moist cake that you can make in just 10 minutes. You probably even already have these six ingredients in your pantry. I highly recommend adding a scoop of ice cream on top.

Recipe: **[Microwave Peanut Butter and Jelly Cake](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/779371876-microwave-peanut-butter-and-jelly-cake)**

## 3\. Matcha Overnight Oats

![An overhead image of a bowl with bright green matcha overnight oats, topped with a dollop of yogurt, raspberries and mango.](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Matcha-overnight-oats-kfpw/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Matcha-overnight-oats-kfpw-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Linda Xiao for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Monica Pierini.

[Overnight oats](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1019516-overnight-oats) and [iced matcha lattes](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1027077-iced-matcha-latte) are a great way to start the day. Ifrah Ahmed combines them into a healthy, make-ahead breakfast that takes only five minutes to prep. The oats can last three to four days in the fridge, so make a batch at the start of the week, and you’ll have morning meals covered. Just roll out of bed, grab a Mason jar and head out the door.

Recipe: **[Matcha Overnight Oats](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1026477-matcha-overnight-oats)**

## 5\. Chicken Salad Slaw With Peanuts and Nori

![A bowl of chicken salad slaw with peanuts and nori. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/08/03/multimedia/03FD-DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-chickensaladslaw-ktvc/03FD-DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-chickensaladslaw-ktvc-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Boring course load? Keep dinner interesting with this satisfying, no-cook salad. It’s creamy, crunchy, salty and fresh all at once. Ali Slagle’s recipe is easier than it looks, thanks to store-bought ingredients like a bag of slaw blend, rotisserie chicken and nori snacks.

## 6\. Cheesy Chile Crisp White Beans

![An overhead image of a cast iron skillet with cheesy chile crisp white beans. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-cheesy-chili-crisp-baked-white-beans-mbtv/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-cheesy-chili-crisp-baked-white-beans-mbtv-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Linda Xiao for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Monica Pierini.

Take a can or two of white beans, some garlic, a little tomato paste and, most importantly, your favorite brand of chile crisp, and bake for a few minutes. Then top with grated Cheddar and bake again until that’s melted. What you end up with is Melissa Clark’s fiery bean bake, which practically begs to be eaten with a bag of tortilla chips.

Recipe: **[Cheesy Chile Crisp White Beans](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1026136-cheesy-chile-crisp-white-beans)**

## 7\. Tuna Mayo Rice Bowl

![An overhead image of a bowl with white rice topped with canned tuna and black sesame seeds.](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Tuna-Rice-Bowl-khcv/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Tuna-Rice-Bowl-khcv-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Eric Kim’s five-minute recipe proves you can make a great meal without ever turning on the stove (the more than 8,000 comments from readers back this up). Grab a can of tuna, some mayo, leftover rice and whatever you want to sprinkle on top. Customizable and filling!

Recipe: **[Tuna Mayo Rice Bowl](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1023224-tuna-mayo-rice-bowl?q=tuna)**

## 8\. Cinnamon Date Smoothie

![Two glasses of cinnamon date smoothie with glass straws. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-jhtp/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-jhtp-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Linda Xiao for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Monica Pierini.

When the craving for a [cinnamon bun](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1024652-homemade-cinnamon-rolls) strikes but you don’t have that kind of time, Sohla El-Waylly’s smoothie is a welcome consolation prize. All you need is a handy-dandy blender, a few ingredients and a few minutes. Buttermilk is an excellent substitute for the tang of cream cheese icing, dates achieve that warm caramel sweetness and nutty flax seed offers that freshly baked aura. And cinnamon, of course.

Recipe: **[Cinnamon Date Smoothie](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1026485-cinnamon-date-smoothie)**

## 11\. Microwave Bacon

![An overhead image of a plate with five strips of cooked bacon.](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Microwave-Bacon-cltp/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Microwave-Bacon-cltp-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Credit... Christopher Testani for The New York Times. Food Stylist: Simon Andrews.

The easiest way to cook bacon is in … the microwave. For real! It’s also the quickest method, according to Genevieve Ko. Microwave on high power for 45 seconds per slice. If you have thick bacon, or if you like it extra crispy, microwave in 30-second bursts until you reach the desired doneness. Just make sure to cover the strips with a paper towel to soak up grease and prevent any splatter.

Recipe: **[Microwave Bacon](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1025759-microwave-bacon)**

## 18\. Microwave-Steamed Eggs

![An overhead image of microwave-steamed eggs topped with soy sauce and chives.](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2025/08/28/multimedia/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Microwave-Steamed-Eggs-pqhw/28DORM-COOKING-ROUNDUP-Microwave-Steamed-Eggs-pqhw-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg?format=pjpg&quality=75&auto=webp&disable=upscale)

Eric Kim’s 10-minute recipe is great for breakfast on its own — drizzled with the soy sauce and maple syrup — or for lunch or dinner alongside steamed rice.

Recipe: **[Microwave-Steamed Eggs](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1022590-microwave-steamed-eggs)**

## 24\. Classic Bean Salad
