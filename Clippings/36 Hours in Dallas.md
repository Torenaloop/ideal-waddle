Millions arrive in Dallas each year for conventions and events, with many more expected this summer for [nine World Cup matches](https://www.dallasfwc26.com/) — a higher number than any other host city. Although the stadium is in Arlington, about 20 miles west of downtown, Dallas will be a hub for watch parties and other events around the tournament. In between matches, there’s plenty to keep you busy: Tex-Mex restaurants everywhere, dive bars with live music, shopping to do and art museums to see. In a metropolitan area that keeps expanding, new bars and restaurants are popping up regularly, particularly around neighborhoods such as Knox-Henderson, the Bishop Arts District and downtown’s East Quarter. Dallas is part of the so-called Metroplex, which includes Fort Worth, but for just a weekend, it’s best to stick with Dallas proper.

## Recommendations

## Itinerary

## Friday

![The interior of a bookstore with wooden furnishings. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-qmgv/28trav-hours-dallas-qmgv-mobileMasterAt3x-v2.jpg)

The Wild Detectives

3:30 p.m. Stroll the shops in the Bishops Arts District

Just southwest of downtown, the Bishop Arts District is easily one of the most walkable neighborhoods. Start at [the Wild Detectives](https://thewilddetectives.com/), a bookstore, bar and coffee shop with a front porch and a large back patio. For a bar without books, try [13 Barrels](https://www.13barrelsdallas.com/), which has rotating Texas beers on draft and cocktails starting at $13, or follow the music, which starts on Friday and weekend afternoons, down the street to [Revelers Hall](https://www.revelershall.com/). [We Are 1976](https://weare1976.com/) has cards, bags, stationery and other knickknacks, as well as prints from independent artists based in Dallas and across the country. There’s also [Spinster Records](https://www.spinsterrecords.com/), which has an impressive vinyl selection and hosts listening parties and D.J. sets.

![The interior of a bookstore with wooden furnishings. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-qmgv/28trav-hours-dallas-qmgv-mobileMasterAt3x-v2.jpg)

The Wild Detectives

6 p.m. Sip on mezcals

While Dallas is known for its Tex-Mex cuisine, focus on the “Mex” side at [Las Almas Rotas](https://lasalmasrotas.com/#) (Spanish for “the broken souls”), just east of downtown and with an impressive selection of tequila and mezcal. To take advantage of the breadth of the spirits, try a flight (ranging from $15 for a flight of three to $40 for a flight of five). The taco plate ($16) comes with four, with options of chicken tinga, carnitas, al pastor, carne asada, beef tongue or veggies. Don't forget the churros ($9) for dessert.

![People sit at mismatched chairs around a wooden table in the patio of a bar. A colorful neon sign reads "Grapevine."](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-hjvm/28trav-hours-dallas-hjvm-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg)

The Grapevine

9 p.m. Take a dive

Dallas has more than its fair share of dive bars. One staple is [the Grapevine](https://www.instagram.com/thegrapevinebar/), which moved by the Southwestern Medical District in 2023 after 27 years in the Oak Lawn neighborhood. It kept its welcoming charm, as well as the basketball hoop, now in its new back patio. Sit on the indoor couches, listen to live music (often a cover band) and play cornhole out back. If you’re looking for another classic Dallas dive bar, head over to [Lee Harvey’s](https://www.leeharveysdallas.com/), in the Cedars neighborhood, which also has live music on weekends.

## Saturday

![A table on a restaurant patio laden with a sandwich, a latte and a flight of mimosas. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-cpzh/28trav-hours-dallas-cpzh-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg)

Halcyon

10 a.m. Caffeinate, then walk through Lower Greenville

In the Lower Greenville neighborhood, start north and work your way down. Grab coffee at the small, European-style [Cafe Duro](https://cafe-duro.com/). Try the McDuro ($10), the cafe’s take on a popular fast-food breakfast sandwich with fennel sausage and on an English muffin. For both a larger space and brunch menu, head over to [Halcyon](https://www.halcyoncoffeebar.com/greenville) for mimosas and breakfast burritos. Keep strolling about 12 minutes south through a residential area to reach the lower part of Lower Greenville, packed with shops, restaurants and bars. Sneakerheads should stop by [the Laboratory DTX](https://thelabdtx.com/), which stocks classics and rare finds. For Dallas merch, check out [Bullzerk](https://www.bullzerk.com/), where you can find hand-printed Dallas T-shirts, hats and your next tote bag. And for Western wear, [Tyler Kingston Mercantile](https://tylerkingston.com/) is a general store with snap shirts and cowboy hats.

![A table on a restaurant patio laden with a sandwich, a latte and a flight of mimosas. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-cpzh/28trav-hours-dallas-cpzh-mobileMasterAt3x.jpg)

Halcyon

1 p.m. Go museum-hopping

You can take your pick in the Dallas Arts District, where three compelling institutions are right next to each other: the [Dallas Museum of Art](https://www.dma.org/), the [Nasher Sculpture Center](https://www.nashersculpturecenter.org/homepage/gad_source/1/gad_campaignid/22233155946?gbraid=0aaaaa-sqh5ueddplwezqxdgo6wj7qz2v4&gclid=cj0kcqjwh-hpbhciarisac0p3cc7yltg6_8tmtryy8riuugukvt7um0zspkupd0hyo5dib1hgk9xyz4aaiqjealw_wcb) and the [Crow Museum of Asian Art](https://crowmuseum.org/). The large Dallas Museum of Art (free, except for special exhibitions) is easy to get lost in, with Greek and Roman sections and a contemporary art gallery. The Nasher’s modern and contemporary sculptures span indoors and outdoors (admission $10). The Crow Museum of Asian Art’s collection includes pottery dating back centuries (free). Once you’ve seen enough of the museum(s), take a short walk up to [Klyde Warren Park](https://www.klydewarrenpark.org/), an urban space on a large deck built directly above the eight lanes of the Woodall Rodgers Freeway that offers great views of the skyline and food trucks on weekends.

![A person looks at a black-and-white photo on display in a museum. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-vfpb/28trav-hours-dallas-vfpb-mobileMasterAt3x-v3.jpg)

The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza

4 p.m. Learn Dallas history

The sixth floor of a former book depository, on the western edge of downtown, is where Lee Harvey Oswald’s rifle was found after President John F. Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas in 1963. The building is now the [Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza](https://www.jfk.org/), and has thousands of objects, films and photos related to the assassination (adult admission, $27). The building is part of a national historic landmark that includes the adjacent Dealey Plaza, where people tried to get a glimpse of President Kennedy as his motorcade made its way through downtown Dallas. From Dealey Plaza, you can see a large X on Elm Street that marks the spot where President Kennedy was shot.

![A person looks at a black-and-white photo on display in a museum. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-vfpb/28trav-hours-dallas-vfpb-mobileMasterAt3x-v3.jpg)

The Sixth Floor Museum at Dealey Plaza

6 p.m. Go for tapas or oysters

Plenty of new restaurants are opening in the neighborhood surrounding Knox Street, but one that has been around for more than 30 years is [Cafe Madrid](https://cafemadrid-dallas.com/), an intimate Spanish tapas spot that feels like having dinner at a friend’s house, if they served Andalusian meatballs ($16), shrimp in olive oil ($14) and Manchego cheese and spinach croquettes ($15). On Knox Street, there is also [Green Point](https://greenpointdallas.com/), a seafood restaurant with oysters (starting at $24 for six), red snapper and shrimp ceviche ($18) and a trio of bite-size fried grouper sandwiches ($23). If you're thirsty, try the Yacht Club, a spin on a white Negroni.

![A band performs on a small stage before a seated audience. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-mctf/28trav-hours-dallas-mctf-mobileMasterAt3x-v3.jpg)

The Balcony Club

9 p.m. Catch some jazz, then knock down some pins

End your night with live music at the speakeasy-esque [Balcony Club](https://www.balconyclub.com/), open since 1988 in a loft space above an old movie theater in the Lakewood neighborhood. The small venue fills up quickly ($20 cover on Friday and Saturday nights), although people filter out between sets of jazz, blues or classic rock. You can also spend some time downstairs at [Bowlski’s](https://www.bowlskisdallas.com/), which has been turned into a bowling alley (lanes start at $16 per adult per hour) with darts, pool and a bar.

![A band performs on a small stage before a seated audience. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-mctf/28trav-hours-dallas-mctf-mobileMasterAt3x-v3.jpg)

## Sunday

![Rows of colorful macarons on display. ](https://static01.nyt.com/images/2026/05/28/multimedia/28trav-hours-dallas-vbmq/28trav-hours-dallas-vbmq-mobileMasterAt3x-v2.jpg)

Chelles Macarons

10 a.m. Shop local and brunch

On weekends at the [Dallas Farmers Market](https://dallasfarmersmarket.org/) in downtown, find in-season produce from area ranchers and farmers under an open-air pavilion with misting fans when it’s hot. If you're flying out and fresh produce is not what you're looking for, you can still grab a bite inside the food hall (open daily). Get your caffeine fix at the [Palmieri Cafe](https://www.palmiericafe.com/), which roasts its own coffee, and some housemade treats at [Chelles Macarons](https://www.chellesmacarons.com/). After shopping, share some small bites at [Sor Juana](https://www.sorjuanatx.com/), a brand-new restaurant that’s like stepping into an old-school Mexican cantina.

Katy Trail

12 p.m. Follow the trail to a Texan ice house

Before it was one of the city’s most popular walking paths, the [Katy Trail](https://katytraildallas.org/) used to be a part of the Missouri-Kansas-Texas railroad built by Union Pacific Railroad. The route was dubbed the “K-T” and later just “the Katy.” It spans about 3.5 miles, from near the American Airlines Center to just south of Southern Methodist University. Most of the largely shaded, concrete trail, which passes the apartment patios of Uptown, also has a padded running track. Along it is the [Katy Trail Ice House](https://katyicehouse.com/), a massive beer garden with dozens of beers on tap served in goblets. The ice house (the term is a nod to long ago when Texans would pick up their ice at these joints), also has burgers, hot dogs, sandwiches and some appetizers to remember Texas by, like the jalapeño bottle caps (fried jalapeño slices) for $14.
