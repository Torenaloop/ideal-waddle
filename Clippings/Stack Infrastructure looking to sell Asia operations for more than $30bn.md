## Stack Infrastructure looking to sell Asia operations for more than $30bn - report

Blue Owl Capital-owned data center firm Stack Infrastructure is reportedly considering the sale of its Asia operations, in a transaction valued at more than $30 billion.

Reporting from [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-06/blue-owl-data-center-operator-weighs-options-including-30-billion-asia-sale), citing sources familiar with the matter, revealed that the company has had conversations with prospective advisers about the partial or full sale of its Australia, Japan, and Malaysia assets.

![stack johor](https://media.datacenterdynamics.com/media/images/stack_johor.width-358.png)

Plans for Stack's Johor Bahru, Malaysia, campus – Stack Infrastructure

No final decisions have been made, and considerations are still in preliminary stages.

The companies did not provide comment to Bloomberg.

Stack Infrastructure was created [in 2019](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ipi-launches-stack-infrastructure-wholesale-provider-eight-data-centers/) by investment firm IPI Partners through a merger of assets originally belonging to Infomart and T5. IPI was acquired by Blue Owl Capital in 2024.

The company first announced plans to expand into APAC [in 2021](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/stack-infrastructure-to-expand-into-apac/). According to Stack’s website, it operates multiple data centers in APAC: one in Malaysia (216MW), two in Japan (114MW), and three in Australia (792MW).

Last month, Stack’s owner, Blue Owl Capital, imposed a cap on withdrawals from two of its key funds following a surge in redemption requests totaling $5.4bn, with investors trying to take back 21.9 percent of Blue Owl’s $20bn Credit Income Corp fund and 40.7 percent of its $3bn tech lending fund, [according to The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/apr/02/blue-owl-capital-private-credit-investment-limits-withdrawals).

Firms like Blue Owl are suffering from a recent market downturn, leading some investors to pull back from risky investments that are particularly exposed to AI advancements.
