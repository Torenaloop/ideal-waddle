**Snowdonia**, or ***Eryri*** (Welsh:[\[ɛrəri\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:IPA/Welsh "Help:IPA/Welsh")), is a mountainous region and [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_the_United_Kingdom "National parks of the United Kingdom") in [North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales "North Wales"). It contains all 15 mountains in Wales [over 3000 feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_3000s "Welsh 3000s") high, including the country's highest, [Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon "Snowdon") (*Yr Wyddfa*), which is 1,085 metres (3,560 ft) tall. These peaks are all part of the [Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_Massif "Snowdon Massif"), [Glyderau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyderau "Glyderau"), and [Carneddau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carneddau "Carneddau") ranges in the north of the region. The lower [Moelwynion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moelwynion "Moelwynion") and [Moel Hebog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moel_Hebog "Moel Hebog") ranges lie immediately to the south.

The national park has an area of 823 square miles (2,130 km <sup>2</sup>) (the fourth-largest in the UK), and covers most of central and southern [Gwynedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd "Gwynedd") and the western part of [Conwy County Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough "Conwy County Borough"). This is much larger than the area traditionally considered Snowdonia, and in addition to the five ranges above includes the [Rhinogydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinogydd "Rhinogydd"), [Cadair Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadair_Idris "Cadair Idris"), and [Aran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aran_Fawddwy "Aran Fawddwy") ranges and the [Dyfi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyfi_hills "Dyfi hills"). It also includes most of the coast between [Porthmadog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porthmadog "Porthmadog") and [Aberdyfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdyfi "Aberdyfi"). The park was the first of the three [national parks of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_Wales "National parks of Wales") to be designated, in October 1951, and the third in the UK after the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District "Peak District") and [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District "Lake District"), which were established in April and May 1951 respectively. The park received 3.89 million visitors in 2015.[^2]

## Toponymy

The name "Snowdon" means "snow hill" and is derived from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English "Old English") elements *"snāw* " and *"dūn* ", the latter meaning "hill".[^3] It is first recorded as *Snawdune* in 1095.[^4] "Snowdonia" is derived from the name of the mountain and is first recorded in 1284, but there is no evidence it survived in common use until it was re-popularised in the nineteenth century.[^4] [^5] The Welsh name of the mountain is **Yr Wyddfa**, and is unrelated to the name of the area.

*Eryri* is first recorded in 1191. It likely derived from *eryr*, meaning "ridge", with the collective form *eryri* referring to the area around Snowdon. A popular interpretation is that the name means "place of the eagle", as *eryr* also means "eagle" and the two meanings are probably [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate "Cognate").[^4] [^6] [^7] [^8] [^9]

## Extent

Before the boundaries of the national park were designated, "Snowdonia" was generally used to refer to a smaller upland area of northern [Gwynedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd "Gwynedd") centred on the Snowdon [massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif "Massif"). The national park covers an area more than twice that size, extending south into the [Meirionnydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meirionnydd "Meirionnydd") area.

This difference is apparent in books published before 1951. In [George Borrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Borrow "George Borrow") 's 1862 *Wild Wales* he states that "Snowdon or Eryri is no single hill, but a mountainous region, the loftiest part of which \[is\] called Y Wyddfa", making a distinction between the summit of the mountain and the surrounding massif.[^10] *The Mountains of Snowdonia* by H. Carr & G. Lister (1925) defines "Eryri" as "composed of the two [cantrefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantref "Cantref") of [Arfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantref_Arfon "Cantref Arfon") and [Arllechwedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arllechwedd "Arllechwedd"), and the two commotes of Nant Conwy and [Eifionydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eifionydd "Eifionydd") ",[^11] which corresponds to [Caernarfonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfonshire "Caernarfonshire") with the exception of southwest [Llŷn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ll%C5%B7n_Peninsula "Llŷn Peninsula") and the [Creuddyn Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creuddyn_Peninsula "Creuddyn Peninsula").

In [*Geography*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_\(journal\) "Geography (journal)") *Volume 26*, a journal of the [Geographical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_Association "Geographical Association") published in 1941, Thomas Cotterill Warrington, writes a section titled "Nomenclature in the Mountains of Carnarvonshire" defining both "Snowdonia" and "Eryri".[^12] "Snowdonia" was described by Warrington as a "tourist's or mountaineer's term" which encompassed the district containing [Pen-y-Gwryd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen-y-Gwryd "Pen-y-Gwryd") at its centre. Its boundaries were described as stretching from the [Conwy valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_valley "Conwy valley") to Fairy Glen gorge (near [Betws-y-Coed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betws-y-Coed "Betws-y-Coed")), then from the [Lledr Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lledr_Valley "Lledr Valley") towards [Dolwyddelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolwyddelan "Dolwyddelan"), and then across the hills to the valley of [Ffestiniog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ffestiniog "Ffestiniog") and to [Porthmadog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porthmadog "Porthmadog") (Portmadoc), before following the road to [Caernarfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon "Caernarfon") passing [Dolbenmaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolbenmaen "Dolbenmaen") and [Llanllyfni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanllyfni "Llanllyfni").[^12] "Eryri" was defined by Warrington to encompass all of what he described as "Snowdonia" plus adding the highlands between [Bwlch Mawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwlch_Mawr "Bwlch Mawr") and [Yr Eifl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yr_Eifl "Yr Eifl").[^12]

In the 1946 book *National Parks for Britain*, by Henry Chessell, he stated that the term *Snowdonia* was a [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") term dating to [Edward I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I_of_England "Edward I of England") and applied to the "mountain district of [Carnarvonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnarvonshire "Carnarvonshire") ". Which encompasses the masses of [Snowdon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_Massif "Snowdon Massif"), the [Carneddau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carneddau "Carneddau"), [Glyderau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyderau "Glyderau"), [Moel Hebog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moel_Hebog "Moel Hebog"), as well as the mountains extending from [Moel Siabod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moel_Siabod "Moel Siabod") forming the [Moelwynion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moelwynion "Moelwynion") which was in neighbouring [Merionethshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merionethshire "Merionethshire"). The [Nant Ffrancon Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nant_Ffrancon_Pass "Nant Ffrancon Pass"), [Llanberis Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanberis_Pass "Llanberis Pass"), the glen between Siabod, the Glyderau and [Capel Curig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capel_Curig "Capel Curig"), and the [Gwynant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynant "Gwynant") valley to [Beddgelert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beddgelert "Beddgelert"), separates those mountain groups. Snowdonia was also suggested to extend further south into [Merionethshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merionethshire "Merionethshire"), with this part bounded by the north by [Afon Glaslyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Glaslyn "Afon Glaslyn"), the glen between [Dolgellau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgellau "Dolgellau") and [Trawsfynydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawsfynydd "Trawsfynydd") to the east, [Cadair Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadair_Idris "Cadair Idris") (overlooking the [Mawddach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mawddach "Mawddach") estuary) to the south and the coast to the west.[^13]

In *Snowdonia: The National Park of North Wales* (1949), F. J. North states that "When the Committee delineated provisional boundaries, they included areas some distance beyond Snowdonia proper".[^14]

## National park

**Eryri National Park** ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language "Welsh language"): *Parc Cenedlaethol Eryri*), originally named **Snowdonia National Park**, was established in October 1951 under the [National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Parks_and_Access_to_the_Countryside_Act_1949 "National Parks and Access to the Countryside Act 1949"). The 1947 Hobhouse Report, the precursor to the Act, recommended the creation of a "North Wales National Park" with boundaries similar to those ultimately adopted, but including the area around [Lake Vyrnwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Vyrnwy "Lake Vyrnwy") and excluding the entire coastal strip from [Harlech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlech "Harlech") to [Barmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmouth "Barmouth").[^15] Eryri was the third [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_parks_of_the_United_Kingdom "National parks of the United Kingdom") to be established the United Kingdom, following the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District "Peak District") and [Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_District "Lake District") in April and May 1951.

The park has an area 823 square miles (2,130 km <sup>2</sup>), covering much of central and southern [Gwynedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd "Gwynedd") and the western part of [Conwy County Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough "Conwy County Borough"), and has 23 miles (37 km) of coastline.[^16] The Hobhouse Report recommended against including the town of [Blaenau Ffestiniog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaenau_Ffestiniog "Blaenau Ffestiniog") in the national park owing to the "disfigurement" caused by its [slate quarries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_industry_in_Wales "Slate industry in Wales"),[^15] and it forms an [enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave_and_exclave "Enclave and exclave") near the centre of the area. The coastal towns of [Tywyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tywyn "Tywyn") and [Barmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmouth "Barmouth") are also excluded.

The park is governed by a , which was established in 1995 and has 18 members: 9 appointed by [Gwynedd Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd_Council "Gwynedd Council"), 3 by [Conwy County Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_County_Borough_Council "Conwy County Borough Council"), and 6 by the [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government "Welsh Government") to represent the national interest.[^17] The authority's main offices are at [Penrhyndeudraeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrhyndeudraeth "Penrhyndeudraeth"). Unlike national parks in other countries, national parks in the United Kingdom consist of both public and private land under a central planning authority. More than 26,000 people live within the park, of whom 58.6% could speak Welsh in 2011.[^18] While most of the land is either open or mountainous, there is a significant amount of [agricultural activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture "Agriculture") within the park.

The national park authority used the name "Snowdonia" in English-language contexts until, in November 2022, it announced that it would begin a transition to using only "Eryri" in order to prioritise [the Welsh language name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#Use_of_Eryri_in_English) for the area; it also began using *Yr Wyddfa* in preference to "Snowdon" to refer to the mountain.[^19] [^20] Following a two-year transition period, the authority announced in November 2024 that the changes would be kept due to its success in gaining support and its adoption by many businesses and by the media.[^21] The authority also announced that the national park logo would be changed to remove "Snowdonia".[^22]

## Geology

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/95/Slate_quarry_near_Penygroes.jpg/500px-Slate_quarry_near_Penygroes.jpg)

Slate quarry with Mynydd Mawr in the background

The geology of Snowdonia is key to the area's character. [Glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciation "Glaciation") during a succession of ice ages has carved a distinctive rocky landscape from a heavily [faulted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\) "Fault (geology)") and [folded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fold_\(geology\) "Fold (geology)") succession of [sedimentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedimentary_rock "Sedimentary rock") and [igneous rocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock "Igneous rock"). The [last ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period "Last Glacial Period") ended only just over 11,500 years ago, leaving features attractive to visitors, which have also played a part in the development of geological science and continue to provide a focus for educational visits. Visiting [Cwm Idwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cwm_Idwal "Cwm Idwal") in 1841, [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin "Charles Darwin") realised that the landscape was the product of glaciation.[^23] The [bedrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedrock "Bedrock") dates largely from the [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian "Cambrian") and [Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician "Ordovician") periods with [intrusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrusive_rock "Intrusive rock") of Ordovician and [Silurian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silurian "Silurian") age associated with the [Caledonian Orogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caledonian_Orogeny "Caledonian Orogeny"). There are smaller areas of Silurian age sedimentary rocks in the south and northeast and of [Cenozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenozoic "Cenozoic") era strata on the [Cardigan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardigan_Bay "Cardigan Bay") coast, though the latter are concealed by more recent deposits. Low grade [metamorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphism "Metamorphism") of Cambrian and Ordovician [mudstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudstone "Mudstone") has resulted in the [slates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate "Slate"), the extraction of which once formed the mainstay of the area's economy.

## Geography

### Mountains

The principal ranges of the traditional Snowdonia are the [Snowdon massif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_massif "Snowdon massif") itself, the [Glyderau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glyderau "Glyderau"), the [Carneddau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carneddau "Carneddau"), the [Moelwynion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moelwynion "Moelwynion") and the [Moel Hebog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moel_Hebog "Moel Hebog") range. All of Wales' [3000ft mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_3000s "Welsh 3000s") are to be found within the first three of these massifs and are most popular with visitors. To their south within the wider national park are the [Rhinogydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinogydd "Rhinogydd") and the [Cadair Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadair_Idris "Cadair Idris") and [Aran Fawddwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aran_Fawddwy "Aran Fawddwy") ranges. Besides these well-defined areas are a number of mountains which are less readily grouped, though various guidebook writers have assigned them into groups such as the "Arenigs", the "Tarrens" and the "Dyfi hills".[^24]

Snowdon's summit at 1,085 metres (3,560 feet) is the highest in Wales and the highest in Britain south of the Scottish Highlands. At 905 metres (2,969 feet), *Aran Fawddwy* is the highest in Wales outside of northern Snowdonia; Cadair Idris, at 893 metres (2,930 feet), is next in line.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/North_snowdonia_panorama.jpg/1200px-North_snowdonia_panorama.jpg)

Panorama of some of the Snowdon Massif including Snowdon (centre right) taken from Mynydd Mawr. The Glyderau are visible in the distance.

Rivers draining the area directly into [Cardigan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardigan_Bay "Cardigan Bay") are typically short and steep. From north to south they include:

- the *[Glaslyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Glaslyn "Afon Glaslyn")* and *[Dwyryd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Dwyryd "Afon Dwyryd")*, which share a common estuary,
- the [Mawddach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Mawddach "Afon Mawddach") and its tributaries the [Wnion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Wnion "River Wnion") and the [Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Eden "Afon Eden"),
- the smaller [Dysynni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Dysynni "Afon Dysynni")
- and on the park's southern margin the [Dyfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dyfi "River Dyfi").

A series of rivers drain to the north coast. Largest of these is the [Conwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Conwy "River Conwy") on the park's eastern margin, which along with the [Ogwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Ogwen "Afon Ogwen") drains into [Conwy Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_Bay "Conwy Bay"). Further west the [Seiont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Seiont "Afon Seiont") and [Gwyrfai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afon_Gwyrfai "Afon Gwyrfai") empty into the western end of the [Menai Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menai_Strait "Menai Strait"). Part of the east of the national park is within the upper [Dee (Dyfrydwy)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Dee,_Wales "River Dee, Wales") catchment and includes [Bala Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala_Lake "Bala Lake"), the largest natural water body in Wales.

A fuller list of the rivers and tributaries within the area is found at [List of rivers of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Wales "List of rivers of Wales").

Aerial video of parts of Northern Snowdonia (2014)

There are few natural bodies of water of any size in Wales; Snowdonia is home to most. Besides Bala Lake, a few lakes occupy [glacial troughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-shaped_valley "U-shaped valley"), including [Llyn Padarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Padarn "Llyn Padarn") and [Llyn Peris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Peris "Llyn Peris") at [Llanberis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanberis "Llanberis") and [Tal-y-llyn Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tal-y-llyn_Lake "Tal-y-llyn Lake") south of *Cadair Idris*. [Llyn Dinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Dinas "Llyn Dinas"), [Llyn Gwynant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Gwynant "Llyn Gwynant"), and [Llyn Cwellyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Cwellyn "Llyn Cwellyn") to the south and west of Snowdon feature in this category, as do [Llyn Cowlyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Cowlyd "Llyn Cowlyd") and Llyn Ogwen on the margins of the Carneddau. There are numerous small lakes occupying [glacial cirques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_cirque "Glacial cirque"), owing to the intensity of past [glacial action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciation "Glaciation") in Snowdonia. Known generically as [tarns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarn_\(lake\) "Tarn (lake)"), examples are [Llyn Llydaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Llydaw "Llyn Llydaw"), [Glaslyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaslyn "Glaslyn") and Llyn Du'r Arddu on Snowdon, [Llyn Idwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Idwal "Llyn Idwal") within the Glyderau and Llyn Cau on Cadair Idris.

There are two large wholly man-made bodies of water in the area, [Llyn Celyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Celyn "Llyn Celyn") and [Llyn Trawsfynydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Trawsfynydd "Llyn Trawsfynydd"), whilst a number of the natural lakes have had their levels artificially raised. [Marchlyn Mawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marchlyn_Mawr "Marchlyn Mawr") reservoir and Ffestiniog Power Station's Llyn Stwlan are two cases where natural tarns have been dammed as part of [pumped storage hydro-electric schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity "Pumped-storage hydroelectricity").[^25]

A fuller list of the lakes within the area is found at [List of lakes of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lakes_of_Wales "List of lakes of Wales"). In 2023, the park [standardised its Welsh language lake names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_standardised_Welsh_lake_names_in_Snowdonia "List of standardised Welsh lake names in Snowdonia"), to be also used in English.[^26]

### Coast

The national park meets the [Irish Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Sea "Irish Sea") coast within [Cardigan Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardigan_Bay "Cardigan Bay") between the Dovey estuary in the south and the Dwyryd estuary. The larger part of that frontage is characterised by [dune systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dune "Sand dune"), the largest of which are [Morfa Dyffryn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morfa_Dyffryn "Morfa Dyffryn") and [Morfa Harlech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morfa_Harlech "Morfa Harlech"). These two locations have two of the largest [sand/shingle spits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spit_\(landform\) "Spit (landform)") in Wales.[^27] The Mawddach and Dwyryd estuaries form the largest indentations, and have large expanses of [intertidal sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertidal_zone "Intertidal zone") and [coastal marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh "Salt marsh") which are especially important for wildlife: see [#Natural history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/#Natural_history). The northern tip of the national park extends to the north coast of Wales at Penmaen-bach Point, west of Conwy, where precipitous cliffs have led to the road and railway [tunnels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A55_road#Penmaenbach_and_Pen-y-clip_tunnels "A55 road").

### Settlements

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Sunrise_over_Snowdonia._%2815946893737%29.jpg/960px-Sunrise_over_Snowdonia._%2815946893737%29.jpg)

Sunrise over Snowdonia

There are only three towns within the park boundary, though there are several more immediately beyond it. [Dolgellau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgellau "Dolgellau") is the most populous [^28] followed by [Bala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala,_Gwynedd "Bala, Gwynedd") [^29] on the eastern boundary and then [Harlech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlech "Harlech") [^30] overlooking [Tremadog Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremadog_Bay "Tremadog Bay"). More populous than these is the town of Blaenau Ffestiniog, which is within an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave "Exclave"), that is to say it is surrounded by the national park but excluded from it, whilst the towns of [Tywyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tywyn "Tywyn") and [Barmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmouth "Barmouth") on the Cardigan Bay coast are within coastal exclaves. [Llanrwst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanrwst "Llanrwst") [^31] in the east, [Machynlleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machynlleth "Machynlleth") [^32] in the south and [Porthmadog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porthmadog "Porthmadog") [^33] and [Penrhyndeudraeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penrhyndeudraeth "Penrhyndeudraeth") in the west are immediately beyond the boundary but still identified with the park; indeed the last of these hosts the headquarters of the Snowdonia National Park Authority.[^34] Similarly the local economies of the towns of [Conwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy "Conwy"), [Bethesda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethesda,_Gwynedd "Bethesda, Gwynedd"), and [Llanberis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanberis "Llanberis") in the north are inseparably linked to the national park as they provide multiple visitor services. The lower terminus of the [Snowdon Mountain Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_Mountain_Railway "Snowdon Mountain Railway") is at Llanberis. Though adjacent to it, [Llanfairfechan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanfairfechan "Llanfairfechan") and [Penmaenmawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penmaenmawr "Penmaenmawr") are less obviously linked to the park.

There are numerous smaller settlements within the national park: prominent amongst these are the eastern 'gateway' village of [Betws-y-Coed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betws-y-Coed "Betws-y-Coed"), [Aberdyfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdyfi "Aberdyfi") on the Dovey (*Dyfi*) estuary and the small village of [Beddgelert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beddgelert "Beddgelert") each of which attract large numbers of visitors. Other sizeable villages are [Llanuwchllyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanuwchllyn "Llanuwchllyn") at the southwest end of Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid), [Dyffryn Ardudwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyffryn_Ardudwy "Dyffryn Ardudwy"), [Corris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corris "Corris"), [Trawsfynydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trawsfynydd "Trawsfynydd"), [Llanbedr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanbedr "Llanbedr"), [Trefriw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trefriw "Trefriw") and [Dolwyddelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolwyddelan "Dolwyddelan").

### Transport

#### Roads

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/29/Snowdon_from_Capel_Curig_%281133366%29.jpg/500px-Snowdon_from_Capel_Curig_%281133366%29.jpg)

Snowdon from Capel Curig

Six [primary routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_the_United_Kingdom#Classification "Roads in the United Kingdom") serve Snowdonia, the busiest of which is the [A55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A55_road "A55 road"), a dual carriageway which runs along the north coast and provides strategic road access to the northern part of the national park. The most important north–south route within the park is the [A470](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A470_road "A470 road") running from the A55 south past Betws-y-Coed to Blaenau Ffestiniog to Dolgellau. It exits the park a few miles to the southeast near [Mallwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallwyd "Mallwyd"). From Dolgellau, the [A494](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A494_road "A494 road") runs to Bala whilst the [A487](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A487_road "A487 road") connects with Machynlleth. The A487 loops around the northwest of the park from Bangor via Caernarfon to Porthmadog before turning in land to meet the A470 east of [Maentwrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maentwrog "Maentwrog"). The [A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5_road_\(Great_Britain\) "A5 road (Great Britain)") was built as a mail coach road by [Thomas Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Telford "Thomas Telford") between London and [Holyhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holyhead "Holyhead"); it enters the park near [Pentrefoelas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentrefoelas "Pentrefoelas") and leaves it near Bethesda. Other A class roads provide more local links; the [A493](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A493_road "A493 road") down the Dovey valley from Machynlleth and up the coast to Tywyn then back up the Mawddach valley to Dolgellau, the [A496](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A496_road "A496 road") from Dolgellau down the north side of the Mawddach to Barmouth then north up the coast via Harlech to Maentwrog. The [A4212](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4212_road "A4212 road") connecting Bala with Trawsfynydd is relatively modern having been laid out in the 1960s in connection with the construction of [Llyn Celyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Celyn "Llyn Celyn"). Three further roads thread their often twisting and narrow way through the northern mountains; [A4085](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4085_road "A4085 road") links Penrhyndeudraeth with [Caernarfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon "Caernarfon"), the [A4086](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4086_road "A4086 road") links Capel Curig with Caernarfon via Llanberis and the [A498](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A498_road "A498 road") links Tremadog with the A4086 at [Pen-y-Gwryd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen-y-Gwryd "Pen-y-Gwryd"). Other roads of note include that from Llanuwchllyn up Cwm Cynllwyd to [Dinas Mawddwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinas_Mawddwy "Dinas Mawddwy") via the 545 metres (1,788 feet) high pass of [Bwlch y Groes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bwlch_y_Groes "Bwlch y Groes"), the second highest tarmacked public road in Wales and the minor road running northwest and west from Llanuwchllyn towards [Bronaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronaber "Bronaber") via the 531 metres (1,742 feet) high pass of Bwlch Pen-feidiog.

#### Railways

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Snowdonia_national_park_%2850620241681%29.jpg/960px-Snowdonia_national_park_%2850620241681%29.jpg)

Pen-y-Pass, Snowdonia national park road

- The double track [North Wales Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales_Coast_Line "North Wales Coast Line") passes along the northern boundary of the park between Conwy and Bangor, briefly entering it at Penmaen-bach Point, where it is in a tunnel. Stations serve the communities of [Conwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_railway_station "Conwy railway station"), [Penmaenmawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penmaenmawr_railway_station "Penmaenmawr railway station"), [Llanfairfechan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanfairfechan_railway_station "Llanfairfechan railway station") and [Bangor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangor_railway_station_\(Wales\) "Bangor railway station (Wales)").
- The single-track [Conwy Valley Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_Valley_Line "Conwy Valley Line") runs south from [Llandudno Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandudno_Junction_railway_station "Llandudno Junction railway station"), and enters the park north of Betws-y-coed, which is served by a station, then continues west up the Lledr valley by way of further stations at [Pont-y-pant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pont-y-Pant_railway_station "Pont-y-Pant railway station"), [Dolwyddelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolwyddelan_railway_station "Dolwyddelan railway station") and [Roman Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Bridge_railway_station "Roman Bridge railway station"). After passing through a tunnel, the passenger line now terminates at [Blaenau Ffestiniog railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaenau_Ffestiniog_railway_station "Blaenau Ffestiniog railway station"), where it connects with the [Ffestiniog Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ffestiniog_Railway "Ffestiniog Railway").

Before 1961 the route continued as the [Bala and Ffestiniog Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala_and_Ffestiniog_Railway "Bala and Ffestiniog Railway") via Trawsfynydd to Bala, joining another [former route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruabon-Barmouth_line "Ruabon-Barmouth line") along the Dee valley which ran southwest via Dolgellau to join the still extant coastal [Cambrian Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Line "Cambrian Line") south of Barmouth.

- The Pwllheli branch of the Cambrian Line splits from the Aberystwyth branch at [Dovey Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dovey_Junction_railway_station "Dovey Junction railway station") and continues northwards via stations at [Aberdovey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdovey_railway_station "Aberdovey railway station"), [Tywyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tywyn_railway_station "Tywyn railway station"), [Tonfanau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonfanau_railway_station "Tonfanau railway station"), [Llwyngwril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llwyngwril_railway_station "Llwyngwril railway station"), [Fairbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairbourne_railway_station "Fairbourne railway station") and [Morfa Mawddach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morfa_Mawddach_railway_station "Morfa Mawddach railway station") to [Barmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmouth_railway_station "Barmouth railway station") where it crosses the Mawddach estuary by the Grade II\* listed wooden [Barmouth Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmouth_Bridge "Barmouth Bridge"), a structure which also provides for walkers and cyclists. Continuing northwards, further stations serve [Llanaber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanaber_railway_station "Llanaber railway station"), [Tal-y-bont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talybont_railway_station "Talybont railway station"), [Dyffryn Ardudwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyffryn_Ardudwy_railway_station "Dyffryn Ardudwy railway station"), [Llanbedr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanbedr_railway_station "Llanbedr railway station"), [Pensarn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensarn_railway_station "Pensarn railway station") and [Llandanwg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandanwg_railway_station "Llandanwg railway station") before reaching [Harlech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlech_railway_station "Harlech railway station"). [Tygwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tygwyn_railway_station "Tygwyn railway station"), [Talsarnau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talsarnau_railway_station "Talsarnau railway station") and [Llandecwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandecwyn_railway_station "Llandecwyn railway station") stations are the last before the line exits the park as it crosses the Dwyryd estuary via [Pont Briwet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pont_Briwet "Pont Briwet") and turns westwards bound for Pwllheli via Penrhyndeudraeth, Porthmadog and Criccieth.

Many sections of dismantled railway are now used by walking and cycling routes and are described elsewhere. The [Bala Lake Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bala_Lake_Railway "Bala Lake Railway") is a heritage railway which has been established along a section of the former mainline route between Bala and Llanuwchllyn. Other heritage railways occupy sections of former mineral lines, often narrow gauge and are described in a separate section.

#### Buses

The national park is served by a growing bus network, branded *Sherpa'r Wyddfa* (formerly Snowdon Sherpa). The network was relaunched in July 2022 with this new brand to reflect the National Park's promotion of Welsh place names. Thus the publicity and websites for the newly branded service only use these Welsh names, even though the publicity is also directed at monoglot English speakers.

Together with the *[TrawsCymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrawsCymru "TrawsCymru")* network of buses, this provides a car-free option for tourists and locals wishing to travel across the National Park.

## Climate

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9a/Snowdonia_in_Winter%2C_Fujifilm_%2846895439682%29.jpg/500px-Snowdonia_in_Winter%2C_Fujifilm_%2846895439682%29.jpg)

Snowdonia in Winter

Snowdonia is one of the [wettest parts of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_the_United_Kingdom "Climate of the United Kingdom"); [Crib Goch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crib_Goch "Crib Goch") in Snowdonia is the wettest spot in the United Kingdom, with an average rainfall of 4,473 millimetres (176.1 in) a year over the 30-year period prior to the mid-2000s.[^35] [^36] (There is a rainfall gauge at 713 metres (2,339 feet) on the slopes below Crib Goch.) [^37]

## History

The earliest evidence for human occupation of the area dates from around 4000–3000 BC with extensive traces of prehistoric [field systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_system "Field system") evident in the landscape. Within these are traces of irregular enclosures and [hut circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_circle "Hut circle"). There are burial chambers of [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic "Neolithic") and [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age "Bronze Age") such as [Bryn Cader Faner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Cader_Faner "Bryn Cader Faner") and [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age "Iron Age") [hillforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillfort "Hillfort") such as [Bryn y Castell](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bryn_y_Castell&action=edit&redlink=1 "Bryn y Castell (page does not exist)") near Ffestiniog.[^38]

The region was finally conquered by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain "Roman Britain") by AD 77–78. Remains of Roman [marching camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra "Castra") and [practice camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra "Castra") are evident. There was a [Roman fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castra "Castra") and [amphitheatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_amphitheatre "Roman amphitheatre") at [Tomen y Mur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomen_y_Mur "Tomen y Mur"). Roads are known to have connected with [Segontium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segontium "Segontium") (Caernarfon) and [Deva Victrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deva_Victrix "Deva Victrix") (Chester) and include the northern reaches of [Sarn Helen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarn_Helen "Sarn Helen").[^39]

There are numerous memorial stones of [Early Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christian "Early Christian") affinity dating from the post-Roman period. The post-Roman hillfort of [Dinas Emrys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinas_Emrys "Dinas Emrys") also dates to this time. Churches were introduced to the region in the 5th and 6th centuries. [Llywelyn the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llywelyn_the_Great "Llywelyn the Great") and [Llywelyn ap Gruffudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llywelyn_ap_Gruffudd "Llywelyn ap Gruffudd") had various stone castles constructed to protect their borders and trade routes. [Edward I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_I "Edward I") built several castles around the margins including those at Harlech and Conwy for military and administrative reasons. Most are now protected within a [World Heritage Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site"). Some of Snowdonia's many stone walls date back to this period too.[^40] In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages "Middle Ages"), the title *Prince of Wales and Lord of Snowdonia* (*Tywysog Cymru ac Arglwydd Eryri*) was used by [Llywelyn ap Gruffudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llywelyn_ap_Gruffudd "Llywelyn ap Gruffudd"); his grandfather [Llywelyn Fawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llywelyn_Fawr "Llywelyn Fawr") used the title *Prince of north Wales and Lord of Snowdonia*.

The 18th century saw the start of industrial exploitation of the area's resources, assisted by the appearance in the late part of the century of [turnpike trusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnpike_trust "Turnpike trust") making it more accessible. The engineer [Thomas Telford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Telford "Thomas Telford") left a legacy of road and railway construction in and around Snowdonia. A new harbour at Porthmadog linked to slate quarries at Ffestiniog via a narrow gauge railway. At its peak in the 19th century the [slate industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_industry_in_Wales "Slate industry in Wales") employed around 12,000 men. A further 1000 were employed in stone quarrying at Graiglwyd and Penmaenmawr. Mining for [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_Wales "Mining in Wales"), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron "Iron") and [gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_gold "Welsh gold") was undertaken during the 18th and 19th centuries, leaving a legacy of mine and mill ruins today.[^41] Ruins of the gold industry are found at [Cefn Coch](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Cefn_Coch_\(goldmine\)&action=edit&redlink=1 "Cefn Coch (goldmine) (page does not exist)") on the [Dolmelynllyn estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmelynllyn_Estate "Dolmelynllyn Estate").[^42]

The [Snowdonia Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdonia_Society "Snowdonia Society") is a registered charity formed in 1967; it is a voluntary group of people with an interest in the area and its protection.

In 1972, one of the two names [Caernarvon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon "Caernarfon") Borough Council suggested, for the unnamed [third district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Wales "Districts of Wales") of Gwynedd, was "Eryri". Its other proposed name would later be chosen as the district of [Arfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arfon_\(district\) "Arfon (district)").[^43]

[Amory Lovins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amory_Lovins "Amory Lovins") led the successful 1970s opposition to stop [Rio Tinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Tinto_Group "Rio Tinto Group") digging up the area for a massive mine.[^44]

## Natural history

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Rain_coming_in_over_the_lake.jpg/500px-Rain_coming_in_over_the_lake.jpg)

Rain coming in over Llyn Cowlyd north of Capel Curig

The park's entire coastline is a [Special Area of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Area_of_Conservation "Special Area of Conservation"), which runs from the [Llŷn Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ll%C5%B7n_Peninsula "Llŷn Peninsula") down the mid-Wales coast, the latter containing valuable sand dune systems.

The park's natural forests are of the mixed [deciduous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deciduous "Deciduous") type, the commonest tree being the [Welsh oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_oak_\(tree\) "Welsh oak (tree)"). [Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch "Birch"), ash, mountain-ash and hazel are also common. The park also contains some large (planted) [coniferous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coniferous "Coniferous") forested areas such as [Gwydir Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwydir_Forest "Gwydir Forest") near [Betws-y-Coed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betws-y-Coed "Betws-y-Coed"), although some areas, once harvested, are now increasingly being allowed to regrow naturally.

### Flora

Northern Snowdonia is the only place in Britain where the [Snowdon lily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_lily "Snowdon lily") (*Gagea serotina*), an [arctic–alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic%E2%80%93alpine "Arctic–alpine") plant, is found and the only place in the world where the Snowdonia hawkweed *[Hieracium snowdoniense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieracium_snowdoniense "Hieracium snowdoniense")* grows.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Llyn_y_Dywarchen.jpg/500px-Llyn_y_Dywarchen.jpg)

Llyn y Dywarchen, near Rhyd Ddu

One of the major problems facing the park in recent years has been the growth of *[Rhododendron ponticum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron_ponticum "Rhododendron ponticum")*.[^45] This fast-growing [invasive species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasive_species "Invasive species") has a tendency to take over and stifle native species. It can form massive towering growths and has a companion fungus that grows on its roots producing toxins that are poisonous to any local flora and fauna for a seven-year period after the *Rhododendron* infestations have been eradicated. As a result, there are a number of desolate landscapes.

### Fauna

Mammals in the park include [otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_otter "Eurasian otter"), [polecats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_polecat "European polecat"), [feral goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_goat "Feral goat"), and [pine martens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_marten "Pine marten").[^46] Birds include [raven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_raven "Common raven"), [red-billed chough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-billed_chough "Red-billed chough"), [peregrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon "Peregrine falcon"), [osprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osprey "Osprey"), [merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_\(bird\) "Merlin (bird)") and the [red kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_kite "Red kite"). The rainbow-coloured [Snowdon beetle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_beetle "Snowdon beetle") (*[Chrysolina cerealis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysolina_cerealis "Chrysolina cerealis")*) is only found in northern Snowdonia.

The feral goats found in Snowdonia are adapted to the rough and steep terrain. Thought to be descended from domesticated herds of goats, which were brought to the area by Neolithic farmers approximately 5,000 years ago.[^47] The goats are said to have become feral after being left and abandoned by farmers and now live in small herds.[^48] One herd that reside above Llanberis consists of more than 50 goats as of 2003, despite a cull in 2006 to control their population.[^49]

### Conservation designations

Snowdonia has a particularly high number of protected sites in respect of its diverse ecology; nearly 20% of its total area is protected by UK and European law.[^50] Half of that area was set aside by the government under the European Habitats Directive as a Special Area of Conservation.[^51] There are a large number of [Sites of special scientific interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Site_of_special_scientific_interest "Site of special scientific interest") (or 'SSSIs'), designated both for fauna and flora but also in some cases for geology. Nineteen of these sites are managed as [national nature reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_nature_reserve_\(United_Kingdom\) "National nature reserve (United Kingdom)") by [Natural Resources Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Resources_Wales "Natural Resources Wales"). The park also contains twelve [Special Areas of Conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Area_of_Conservation "Special Area of Conservation") (or 'SACs'), three [Special Protection Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Protection_Area "Special Protection Area") (or 'SPAs') and three [Ramsar sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention "Ramsar Convention").[^51] Some are wholly within the park boundaries, others straddle it to various degrees.[^52]

There are numerous SSSIs within the park, the most extensive of which are Snowdonia, Migneint-Arenig-Dduallt, Morfa Harlech, Rhinog, Berwyn, Cadair Idris, Llyn Tegid, Aber Mawddach / Mawddach Estuary, Dyfi, Morfa Dyffryn, Moel Hebog, Coedydd Dyffryn Ffestiniog and Coedydd Nanmor.

The following NNRs are either wholly or partly within the park:Allt y Benglog, Y Berwyn (in multiple parts), Cader Idris, Ceunant Llennyrch, Coed Camlyn, Coed Cymerau, Coed Dolgarrog, Coed Ganllwyd, Coed Gorswen, Coed Tremadog, Coedydd Aber, Coedydd Maentwrog (in 2 parts), Coed y Rhygen, Cwm Glas Crafnant, Cwm Idwal, Hafod Garregog, Morfa Harlech, Rhinog and Snowdon.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Eryri_mountains_reflection_%2850879310288%29.jpg/500px-Eryri_mountains_reflection_%2850879310288%29.jpg)

Eryri mountains reflection

The twelve SACs are as follows:

- Afon Eden – Cors Goch Trawsfynydd
- Afon Gwyrfai a (*and*) Llyn Cwellyn
- Part of the Berwyn a Mynyddoedd De Clwyd / Berwyn and South Clwyd Mountains SAC.
- Cadair Idris (in two parts)
- Coedydd Derw a Safleoedd Ystlumod Meirion / Meirionydd Oakwoods and Bat Sites: a series of sites between Tremadog, Trawsfynydd, and Ffestiniog and Beddgelert and extending up the Gwynant. It also includes many of the oakwoods of the Mawddach and its tributaries.
- Corsydd Eifionydd (*Eifionydd Fens*) (north of Garndolbenmaen)
- Eryri/Snowdonia, which covers much of the Carneddau, Glyderau, and the Snowdon massif
- Migneint-Arenig-Dduallt
- Mwyngloddiau Fforest Gwydir / Gwydyr Forest Mines (north of Betws-y-Coed)
- The Pen Llyn a'r Sarnau / Lleyn Peninsula and the Sarnau SAC covers the entire Cardigan Bay coastline of the park and the sea area and extends above the high water mark at Morfa Harlech, Mochras and around the Dovey and Mawddach estuaries.
- Rhinog
- River Dee and Bala Lake / Afon Dyfrdwy a Llyn Tegid

The three SPAs are Dovey Estuary / Aber Dyfi (of which a part is within the park), Berwyn (of which a part is within the park) and Migneint-Arenig-Dduallt.

#### Ramsar sites

The three designated [Ramsar sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_site "Ramsar site") are the [Dyfi Biosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyfi_Biosphere "Dyfi Biosphere") (Cors Fochno and Dyfi), Cwm Idwal and Llyn Tegid (Bala Lake).

## Economy

The area's economy was traditionally centred upon farming and from the early 19th century increasingly on mining and quarrying. Tourism has become an increasingly significant part of Snowdonia's economy during the 20th and 21st centuries.

### Hill farming

The extensive farming of [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep "Sheep") remains central to Snowdonia's farming economy.

### Forestry

Significant sections of the park were afforested during the 20th century for timber production. Major [conifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer "Conifer") plantations include [Dyfi Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyfi_hills#Forestry "Dyfi hills"), [Coed y Brenin Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coed-y-Brenin "Coed-y-Brenin") [^53] between Dolgellau and Trawsfynydd, [Penllyn Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Penllyn_Forest&action=edit&redlink=1 "Penllyn Forest (page does not exist)") south of Bala, [Beddgelert Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Beddgelert_Forest&action=edit&redlink=1 "Beddgelert Forest (page does not exist)") [^54] and [Gwydyr (or Gwydir) Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwydir_Forest "Gwydir Forest") near Betws-y-Coed which is managed as a [forest park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park#Forest_park "Park") by [Natural Resources Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Resources_Wales "Natural Resources Wales").[^55]

### Slate industry

The region was once the most important producer of [slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate "Slate") in the world. Some production continues but at a much reduced level from its peak. The park boundaries are drawn such that much of the landscape affected by slate quarrying and mining lies immediately outside of the designated area.

### Energy production

Construction of a [nuclear power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_station "Nuclear power station") beside [Llyn Trawsfynydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyn_Trawsfynydd "Llyn Trawsfynydd") began in 1959 with the first power produced in 1965. The site was operational until 1991 though it continues as an employer during its decommissioning phase. Pumped storage hydroelectric schemes are in operation at Llanberis and Ffestiniog.

### Tourism

Research indicates that there were 3.67 million visitors to Snowdonia National Park in 2013, with approximately 9.74 million tourist days spent in the park during that year.[^56] Total tourist expenditure was £433.6 million in 2013.[^56]

#### Hiking

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Badgernet_Snowdonia_walks_1.JPG/500px-Badgernet_Snowdonia_walks_1.JPG)

Southern edge. Waymarked path near Llyn Barfog in Gwynedd

Many of the [hikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiking "Hiking") in the area concentrate on Snowdon itself. It is regarded as a fine mountain, but at times gets very crowded;[^57] [^58] in addition the [Snowdon Mountain Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdon_Mountain_Railway "Snowdon Mountain Railway") runs to the summit.[^59]

The other high mountains with their boulder-strewn summits as well as [Tryfan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryfan "Tryfan"), one of the few mountains in the UK south of Scotland whose ascent needs hands as well as feet are also very popular. However, there are also some spectacular walks in Snowdonia on the lower mountains, and they tend to be relatively unfrequented. Among hikers' favourites are [Y Garn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Garn_\(Glyderau\) "Y Garn (Glyderau)") (east of Llanberis) along the ridge to [Elidir Fawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elidir_Fawr "Elidir Fawr"); Mynydd Tal-y-Mignedd (west of Snowdon) along the [Nantlle Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nantlle_Ridge "Nantlle Ridge") to Mynydd Drws-y-Coed; [Moelwyn Mawr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moelwyn_Mawr "Moelwyn Mawr") (west of Blaenau Ffestiniog); and [Pen Llithrig y Wrach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_Llithrig_y_Wrach "Pen Llithrig y Wrach") north of [Capel Curig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capel_Curig "Capel Curig"). Further south are [Y Llethr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y_Llethr "Y Llethr") in the [Rhinogydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinogydd "Rhinogydd"), and [Cadair Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadair_Idris "Cadair Idris") near [Dolgellau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolgellau "Dolgellau").

The park has 1,479 miles (2,380 km) of public footpaths, 164 miles (264 km) of public [bridleways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridleway "Bridleway"), and 46 miles (74 km) of other public rights of way.[^60] A large part of the park is also covered by [right to roam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_roam "Right to roam") laws.

#### Recreational routes

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Sunset_over_Snowdonia%26Padarn_country_park._%2816661612713%29.jpg/500px-Sunset_over_Snowdonia%26Padarn_country_park._%2816661612713%29.jpg)

Sunset over Snowdonia & Padarn country park

The [Wales Coast Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_Coast_Path "Wales Coast Path") runs within the park between Machynlleth and Penrhyndeudraeth, save for short sections of coast in the vicinity of Tywyn and Barmouth which are excluded from the park. It touches the park boundary again at Penmaen-bach Point on the north coast. An inland alternative exists between Llanfairfechan and Conwy, wholly within the park. The [North Wales Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales_Path "North Wales Path"), which predates the WCP, enters the park north of Bethesda and follows a route broadly parallel to the north coast visiting [Aber Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aber_Falls "Aber Falls") and the [Sychnant Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sychnant_Pass "Sychnant Pass") before exiting the park on the descent from [Conwy Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conwy_Mountain "Conwy Mountain"). The [Cambrian Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Way "Cambrian Way") is a long-distance trail between Cardiff and Conwy that stays almost entirely within the national park [^61] from [Mallwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallwyd "Mallwyd") northwards.[^62] It was officially recognised in 2019,[^63] and is now depicted on [Ordnance Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey "Ordnance Survey") maps.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3b/Eryri_meaning_sign.jpg/375px-Eryri_meaning_sign.jpg)

A sign in Hafod Eryri, Snowdon's summit building, giving the meaning of the name Eryri

There have been calls to use the Welsh name for the area, *Eryri*, in preference to "Snowdonia" in English-language contexts. In 2003, the pressure group [Cymuned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymuned "Cymuned") campaigned for such a change, inspired by other campaigns such as Ayers Rock being known as [Uluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru "Uluru") and [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest") as Qomolangma.[^64] In 2020, an [e-petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-petition "E-petition") calling for the removal of the English name was put forward to the [Senedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd "Senedd"), but rejected as responsibility lies with the Snowdonia .[^65] In 2021, an [e-petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-petition "E-petition") on the same topic attracted more than 5,300 signatures and was presented to the national park authority.[^66] [^67] The authority was already considering its language policy, but stated that the petition encouraged it to take "decisive action", and it resolved to use Welsh names as far as legally possible in November 2022;[^68] it must continue to use "Snowdonia" in statutory documents.[^69] [^70] Before the decision, the authority had already prioritised the Welsh names by using them first and giving the English names in parentheses.[^71] [^72] The naming policy applies only to the national park authority.[^73]

In November 2024, the authority stated that the name change had been a "success" as many businesses and media had followed suit. The authority stated that a summer survey showed "strong support" from locals and visitors for the name change. While some concerns were raised over misconceptions by some that the Welsh names were new and mispronunciations, the authority stated that the change resulted in the park being more associated with a Welsh identity, and more different from other UK national parks. The authority is set to make a pronunciation guide and update its logo which still contains Snowdonia.[^21] Two days later the authority revealed the new logo that removed the name "Snowdonia".[^74]
