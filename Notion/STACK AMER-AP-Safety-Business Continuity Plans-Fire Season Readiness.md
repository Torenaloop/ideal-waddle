# Purpose

1. The purpose of this Fire Season Readiness Procedure is to establish minimum requirements for fire season readiness in STACK Infrastructure Data Centers. This procedure defines the process for operating STACK facilities before, during, and after fire season to ensure personnel safety and business continuity.

# Key Responsibilities

2. The Critical Operations Manager is responsible for:
    1. assisting managers and supervisors in preparing and responding to wildfire and air quality concerns reported by personnel during wildfire season;
    2. ensuring Fire Season Readiness and Response is maintained and followed in accordance with current recommendations published by Oregon Department of Forestry (ODF), California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CALFIRE), and any other applicable groups or local regulations;
    3. conducting a periodic review of this procedure at least annually, prior to the start of traditional wildfire season, to ensure that it is appropriately maintained;
    4. developing and implementing this Fire Season Readiness and Response Procedure; and
    5. making necessary changes to this and other STACK Infrastructure procedures to correct any systemic issues discovered during preparing and responding to wildfires.
3. Managers and Supervisors are responsible for:
    6. conducting routine visual workplace inspections to confirm that the requirements in this procedure are being followed;
    7. ensuring compliance with all of STACK Infrastructure’s safety policies and written procedures, including this Fire Season Readiness Procedure;
    8. ensuring all air monitors, alarms, and equipment identified in this procedure are on hand, maintained, and in good condition;
    9. ensuring appropriate administrative and engineering controls, including regularly scheduled and corrective maintenance procedures for diesel generator, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems, are in place as needed for ensuring adequate response to wildfires;
    10. ensuring personnel are trained on this procedure and in their responsibilities for preparing and responding when wildfire concerns are presented during wildfire season;
    11. investigating air quality concerns when they are reported and responding appropriately.
4. All personnel, including STACK Infrastructure employees, contractors, subcontractors, and vendors, are responsible for following all STACK Infrastructure safety policies and written procedures, including this Fire Season Readiness Procedure. All personnel are also responsible for:
    12. completing all required Fire Season Readiness training.
    13. informing managers of any planned activities that have the potential to impact the site’s readiness and response to wildfires.
    14. notifying managers and supervisors of any fire readiness concerns and participating in preparation and response to wildfires.
5. **Detailed Process and Policy**
    15. Fire Risk Categorization
        1. Fire Weather Watch Warning: Ensure preparation steps of this AP are started or completed.
        2. Red Flag Warning: Ensure preparation steps of this AP are completed.
        3. Air Quality Index of 101-150: Provide weekly updates to clients.
        4. Air Quality Index >150: Provide daily updates to clients.
    16. Preparation Phase
        5. Supplies
            1. Reference the Stack Standard ‘Emergency Site Preparedness’ AP.
            2. Site Specific Supplies Checklist. This is an in-house designated checklist.
            3. Smoke Eaters for Office Spaces.
        6. Emergency Supplies
            4. Reference the Stack Standard ‘Inclement Weather’ AP.
            5. Reference the Stack Standard ‘Emergency’ AP.
        7. Air Quality
            6. Perform Indoor Air Quality Assessment per the ‘Indoor Air Quality’ AP.
            7. Use air sampling devices
6. Particle Counters
    17. Equipment
        8. Verify generator oil levels and fill if necessary.
        9. Perform APM on generators prior to the start of fire season.
        10. Inspect AHU filters to determine if replacement is necessary prior to the start of fire season.
    18. Supplies
        11. Refer to the Emergency Site Preparedness AP for supplies that will need to be accumulated.
    19. During Elevated Fire Risk
        12. Communication
            8. Critical Operations will open a Sev3 PagerDuty Incident Bridge and escalate to site Leadership when a red flag warning is issued for elevated fire risk.
            9. Site Leadership will notify clients and building occupants of current operational protocol for facility evacuation and staffing guidelines based on fire risk categorization or as required by the client or Leadership.
                1. Air Quality Index of 101-150: Provide weekly updates to clients.
                2. Air Quality Index >150: Provide daily updates to clients.
            10. Site Leadership will communicate Operational status updates to clients per the following notification template:

*Stack’s continued effort to prioritize the health and safety of our clients is again at the forefront due to the current wildfire situation in [location]. Operations has taken the following actions to minimize risk to clients and facility staff:*

*Power: Local utility companies have provided an update on the health of the utility grid [location]. Currently there is no risk to the region. STACK operations have daily check ins with utility providers to discuss any changes in the situation.*

*Generator Fuel: The [location] site currently has 24hrs+ of fuel for each generator. Local fuel vendors have been contacted to ensure we are ready to order fuel if required.*

*Air Quality: Due to the hazardous air quality in the region, STACK operations have adjusted facility make up air intake dampers to minimum positions and closed them where possible. Filter health remains satisfactory. A complete filter change out will be conducted once air quality improves or on an as needed basis.*

*Facility Staffing: Currently there is no impact to staffing levels at the facility. STACK will follow the local/State guidelines for evacuation orders. Currently the [location] region is not under any level of evacuation orders. If required, the site can be monitored remotely by STACK operations.*

7. As wildfire developments continue, site Leadership will update and submit the client notification template on a daily basis to reflect current event transpirations regarding Power, Generator Fuel, Air Quality, and Facility Staffing.
    1. It should be suggested for all building occupants to wear an N95 mask, or better, for traveling to and from the site as fire risk becomes moderate or greater.
8. Site Leadership will communicate with the Utility Company for potential outages.
9. Critical Operations will contact fuel vendors for Diesel fuel resupply as generators are utilized in lieu of unstable Utility power.
10. Execution
    2. Critical Operations staff will conduct continuous facility rounds during elevated fire risk:
        1. Check for fire hazards including indoors, damaged, or dirty AHU filters, VESDA alarms, and flames proximal to the site.
        2. Verify the site is not experiencing any power or BMS anomalies.

> (c) Verify Generators have 24+ hours of fuel.

11. Critical Operations will perform filter change-outs of AHUs if filters are found to be damaged or unsatisfactory.
12. Manually override Outside Air dampers to allow minimal outside air to enter the facility.
    1. Install an air circulation device or smoke eater for increased indoor air quality.
13. If an evacuation order is issued:
    2. Critical Operations will open a Sev1 PagerDuty Incident Bridge for escalation to STACK Leadership.
    3. Critical Operations will remotely monitor the facility and respond on-site on an as needed basis.
14. Recovery Phase
    4. Communication
        1. Verify all regional evacuation orders have been lifted.
        2. COM and ACOM will communicate to local teams when it is safe to return to the building.
        3. COM will communicate with clients a timeline for returning to the building.
    5. Check status of equipment:
        4. Complete detailed site rounds to verify no damage to the building or surrounding grounds.
        5. Inspect air filters for damage and verify pressure DP is within allowable tolerance.
            1. Replace filters if of tolerance or if smoke smell is still noticeable in the building.
            2. Order replacement filters to replenish on-hand stock.
    6. Facility Cleaning
        6. Communicate with a local vendor for interior and exterior building cleaning.
15. **References**
    7. STACK Infrastructure’s Indoor Air Quality AP
    8. STACK Infrastructure’s Emergency Site Preparedness AP
    9. STACK Infrastructure’s Inclement Weather AP

# Definitions

16. Fire Weather Watch Warning – Warning given by the National Weather Service indicating weather conditions that could cause a fire within the next 72 hours.
17. Red Flag Warning – Warning given by the National Weather Service indicating weather conditions that could cause a fire within the next 24 hours.
18. Smoke Eater - A device or piece of equipment designed to remove smoke from the air.
19. Particle Counter (air quality meter) - A particle counter is an instrument that detects and counts particles. A particle counter is a single particle counter, meaning it detects and counts particles one at a time. The nature of particle counting is based upon either light scattering, light obscuration, or direct imaging.
