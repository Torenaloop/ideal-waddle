# Purpose, scope and users

This plan is specifically for STACK Data Center and is designed to comply with U.S. OSHA regulations. Responsibilities are defined for:

- Handling and investigation of emergency accidents, incidents, and nonconformities.
- Taking action to mitigate any related consequences, including the inflation and completion of corrective and preventative actions.
- Confirmation of the effectiveness of actions taken.

It is impossible for any business continuity plan to address all potential scenarios and unforeseen events. However, all emergencies will entail an initial Emergency Response and possibly a declared State of Emergency. Once the State of Emergency concludes; the focus changes from mitigating the emergency to resuming normal business functions.

1. **Definitions**
- Emergency – A sudden, unexpected, or impending situation or event that could cause injury, loss of life, damage to property, and/or significant interference with normal business activities of STACK personnel or company site(s) and therefore requires immediate attention and remedial action.
- Crisis Management Team (CMT) – STACK Data Center Team comprised of various Corporate Executives. The CMT makes decisions regarding the allocation of resources on Regional or National levels. The CMT provides direction to all other STACK employees regarding incident management and business resumption.
    1. **Crisis Types:**
    - Unanticipated System Failure – Failure of known system, but unanticipated origin. No standard response procedures exist.
    - Undefined System Failure – Failure of unknown origin. Response not yet defined.
    - Extended or Compound Failure – Emergency response procedures may exist for the failure and/or its components, but the extent, duration or complexity of the failure is outside the limits of established procedures.
    - Severe Failure – Known failure type that may not respond to established procedures in the prescribed timeframe.
    - Unresponsive Failure – Known failure type that does not respond to established procedures in the prescribed time frame.
    2. **Disaster Types:**
    - Man Made
        - Attack on facility or personnel
        - Equipment fire/explosion
        - Hazardous material release
        - Catastrophic facility failure
    - Natural
        - Severe Weather
        - Earthquake
        - Flood
        - Fire
- Emergency Command Center (ECC) – The ECC is the physical location where the onsite facility teams come together during an emergency to coordinate response and recovery actions and resources.
- Business Recovery Site (Work Area) – Once an incident has stabilized, the direction of recovery efforts will transition from the Emergency Command Center to a Business Recovery Site Work Area.
- Disaster Staging Area – The Disaster Staging Area is a pre-designated place established for temporary location of available emergency responder resources. It can be any location in which personnel, supplies, and equipment can be temporarily housed or parked while awaiting operational assignment. Staging areas are established by the Emergency Response Coordinator (or designee) to enable positioning of and accounting for resources until assigned.
- Emergency Equipment – Emergency Equipment refers to spill control equipment, firefighting equipment, first aid materials, communications equipment, and personal protective equipment (PPE). Automatic sprinkler and fire suppression systems extend throughout the facility. Fire extinguishers are also located throughout the facility. These types are capable of extinguishing common fires including electrical fires and fires involving flammable liquids. They are visually inspected monthly and undergo annual preventative maintenance. The extinguishers are supplied for use by trained personnel and fire department employees only. First aid kits are located on the walls of common areas, and in emergency response bag kits maintained at the Security Post. The wall kits are accessible to all employees, whereas the bag kits are used only by trained Security staff.
- Emergency Floor and Site Maps – Emergency Floor and Site plans will be prepared as part of this Plan. Each map will be appropriately marked with necessary safety/emergency information, such as; utility shut off valves, fire department stand pipes, disaster staging areas, etc.
- Evacuation – An evacuation is the temporary and rapid removal of people from the building or disaster (or threatened) area as a rescue or precautionary measure.
- Press Area – An area, established if necessary, as decided by the Emergency Response Coordinator, where any media communications will be handled by appropriate personnel during and following an emergency event or incident. All media personnel should be directed to this location. Communications with the news media should be directed by STACK Senior Leadership Team.

Employees or contractors assigned to the site should be directed not to make statements unless specifically instructed to do so by STACK Senior Leadership Team.

- Shelter-in-Place/Protect-in-Place is a process for taking immediate shelter in a location readily accessible to the affected individual(s) by sealing off a single area (an example being a room) from outside contaminants and shutting off all HVAC systems. These actions would generally be taken after a chemical accident or terrorist attack. Depending on the situation, everyone within a specific distance of the incident may be ordered to shelter in place or people within a closer range may be ordered to evacuate while everyone else shelters in place. Sheltering in place is generally used for a short period of time.
- Coordination Center – The Coordination center will be established, if deemed necessary by the Emergency Response Coordinator, for the purpose of evacuating muster groups.
- Senior Person Onsite – The person in charge during an incident, after normal business hours when building management personnel are not immediately available. This person may be a member of Technical Operations, Security, or Customer Teams depending on the nature of and proximity to the emergency or incident site.
- Triage Area – The Triage Area will be established as directed by the Emergency Response Coordinator, or Public Authorities and will be an area for triaging the injured into groups based on their need for or likely benefit from immediate medical treatment.

3. **Introduction**

This emergency response and business continuity plan includes processes that help prepare for emergency situations — whether those events might include a natural disaster, such as a hurricane, or simple events, such as a power outage. The processes in this plan provide a path for continued operation in addition to immediate emergency response.

The Emergency Response Plan describes the actions to be taken during a medical emergency, a fire/explosion, a hazardous material release, or other potentially unsafe environmental condition. The responsibilities of Emergency Coordinators are described herein and include emergency response action coordination, emergency equipment maintenance, and plan updates:

- Used as a reference guide during an emergency.
- Present measures to minimize hazards to human health and the environment from fires, explosions, hazardous material releases, and other emergency situations
- Familiarize outside emergency response agencies (e.g., police, fire, rescue, hospital, etc.) with the internal emergency response procedures established at a STACK facility.

The Emergency Response Plan is intended for use by facility personnel to prepare for and respond to emergency conditions. Emergency Coordinators are required to have a working knowledge of the procedures described in the Plan. Also, proper attention to the fundamental response concepts can mitigate emergencies that may develop at STACK. The objectives of the emergency response plan are to:

- Protect human life, health and safety.
- Limit and contain damage to the facility and the equipment therein.
- Effectively manage communications throughout the incident.

The Business Continuity Plan is intended to provide guidance for the quick and effective recovery of critical business functions following any event that causes the loss or limited use of STACK Data Center facilities and services. The objectives of the Business Continuity Plan are to:

- Minimize financial and operational impact resulting from a short term or long-term loss of facilities, technology or communications equipment.
- Prioritize mission critical functions that require immediate restoration.
- Serve as the guiding document for each business unit’s individual business continuity plans.
4. **Scope**

This plan is specifically for STACK Data Center and is prepared to comply with US OHSA regulations. Responsibilities are defined for:

- Handling and investigation of emergency accidents, incidents, and nonconformities.
- Taking action to mitigate any related consequences, including the initiation and completion of corrective and preventive actions.
- Confirmation of the effectiveness of actions taken during an event.
5. **Training**

To ensure the success of the Emergency Response and Business Continuity Plan, all team members must receive training with regard to its contents. The STACK Operations Team, Critical Facility Manager, and/or Security Director are responsible to ensure that emergency training is provided for all team members. Training should occur with regular periodicity to enhance process familiarity. The components of the training program shall include:

- A centrally accessible electronic copy of this plan
- Periodic drills and exercises to ensure employee familiarization with all aspects of this plan.

25. **Pandemic**

The STACK Pandemic Response Plan focuses on operational changes required in response to Pandemic conditions within our operating regions.

According to the CDC (Centres for Disease Control & Prevention), pandemics occur when novel (new) viruses emerge which can infect people easily and spread from person to person in an efficient and sustained way.

### Response Tiers

STACK has established five basic operational tiers to assist in response to pandemic conditions.

- Tier One: Provides for reasonable onsite precautions to be taken by all personnel at a facility, with the aim of limiting the risk of pandemic being experienced by any clients, staff, or vendors.
- Tier Two: Provides for elevated precautions at a facility to reduce the number of visitors to the site and limit the potential of disease transmission.
- Tier Three: Operational level that provides a response to actual experience of contamination or exposure at a site.
- Tier Four: Operational level when limited staff are available for 24/7 site coverage.
- Tier Five: Operational level when site is remotely monitored, and no Critical Operations or Security staff are on site.

Each Response Tier will be utilized based on advice from Authority Having Jurisdiction and will be clearly communicated to all personnel and Clients associated with a facility.

### Tier One (reasonable precautions)

Trigger

- CDC Level 1 (Watch) precautions will be triggered by Federal/State/Local governmental bodies (including the CDC (Centres for Disease Controls & Prevention)) and/or STACK management exercising common sense precautionary measures.
- CDC declaring Public Health Emergency (PHE)

Responses

- Housekeeping:
    - Elevated housekeeping measures will be in effect to provide additional cleaning and disinfectant of all commonly touched surfaces. Notifications to be posted throughout facility with recommendations for hand washing and sanitizing and other measures to reduce risk of infection.
    - Verify stock of hand hygiene supplies, order surplus if supply shortage is anticipated.
- Access Control:
    - Ensure all visitors to site are provided with information about reasonable precautions to avoid spreading infection. Advise all visitors to take precautions when using biometric access control devices by frequently utilizing the hand sanitizer dispensers at high traffic access points.
- On Site activities:
    - Request clients and vendors to reduce time spent on site for non-essential activities. Continue with normal maintenance activities.
- Staffing:
    - Maintain normal staffing levels throughout. Instruct all staff to self-identify if they are at elevated risk due to personal health issues, recent travel or proximity to infection clusters.
    - In the event staff believe they are at risk, follow CDC guidelines on self-isolation and testing.
    - Proactively check on staff daily to ensure that anyone exhibiting symptoms does not come to the facility.

### Tier Two (Elevated precautions)

Triggers

- CDC Level Two (Practice Enhanced Precautions) identification of official pandemic status, advice from local Authority Having Jurisdiction, or Stack Management instruction.
- Documented cases reported in local area or widespread regional outbreak.

Responses

- Housekeeping:
    - Continue elevated housekeeping measures, including multiple daily cleaning and disinfecting of “high touched surfaces” including door handles and bio-metric access control devices.
        - With disinfecting cleaning wipes, wipe down all “high touch surfaces” twice daily minimum.
        - Prior to starting Critical Operations shifts and Security shifts, with disinfecting cleaning wipes, wipe down all shared surfaces that may have been touched by the prior shift. These surfaces include shift phones, common workstations (keyboard/mouse), and common SOC control touch points.
    - Position sanitizing and cleaning wipes throughout common areas for personnel to use.
    - Ensure hand hygiene supplies are kept stocked in all restrooms and kitchenettes.
    - Order further surplus of hand hygiene supplies to anticipate supply chain delays.
- Access Control:
    - Post the Self-Assessment signs at building entrance, person traps, and other high traffic areas.
    - For sites with active construction projects, at a local site level, establish appropriate construction entrance and appropriately isolate construction area, with proper ventilation and filters, to maximize the separation between construction team and Critical Operations/Security Team.
    - Wherever practical, avoid having more than one security guard in the SOC at any one time.
- On Site activities:
    - Postpone all non-critical maintenance activities by third party vendors unless approved by CDCO.
    - Request clients and vendors to avoid visiting the site for non-essential activities.
    - Postpone all sales tours and any other non-essential visits to site unless approved by CRO.
    - Ensure onsite emergency generator diesel fuel supply is optimized.
        - Verify multiple fuel suppliers are available to provide fuel if necessary.
    - If necessary, stress test and/or upgrade VPN’s for increased working-from-home.
    - Use best efforts to maintain at least 6’ distance between yourself and any other onsite personnel.
- Staffing
    - Remind staff that the slightest symptoms require staying home.
    - Notify all personnel that if role does not require to be onsite, coordinate with manager and begin working from home.
    - Enact agreements with additional local partners to provide staff augmentation to ensure required site coverage is met, as necessary.

### Tier Three (On site Contamination/Exposure & Cleaning)

Triggers

- Documented contamination/exposure at a facility.

Responses

STACK Pandemic Response Team determines appropriate response to exposure.

- Conduct contact tracing if possible**.**
- If deemed appropriate by STACK Pandemic Response Team:
    - Evacuate site except for (1) Critical Operations team member and (1) Security team member. To the extent possible, Critical Operations staff team member will not enter potentially contaminated area(s).
    - With remote monitoring, review ability to respond to events with existing team members on property or on adjacent property.
    - Besides (1) Critical Operations team member, the only other individual(s) allowed on site are janitorial team members.

38. **Wildfire**

A term applied to any unwanted, unplanned, damaging fire burning in forest, shrub, or grassland. Wildfires are fast spreading, dangerous and difficult to control and/or extinguish.

### Wildfire Plan

STACK’s business wildfire continuity plan will consist of a three-tier system that establishes readiness levels in order to provide a flexible and coordinated response to escalating threat levels or actual emergencies, focusing on possible threats to our data center facilities.

The tier system is a means to establish, measure and report the readiness of STACK INFRASTRUCTURE’s continuity programs independent of other state or federal government readiness systems.

The three tiers provide for an incremental increase in and deployment of people and resources to enhance staffing, survivability, responsiveness, and availability of assets for immediate support.

STACK will watch closely for any announcements from Federal, State or Local governments regarding evacuations. Most cities and states will have at least a two-phase approach. Phase one consists of preparing to evacuate and phase two is the evacuation. STACK Local staff will monitor the situation and enact STACK’s Business Continuity Plan at a moment’s notice.

### Tier One - Plan Development, Review, Training, and Testing.

Tier One calls for a discussion with designated leadership and continuity staffs about performing the organization’s essential functions from alternate facilities either as a result of, or in preparation for, a catastrophic emergency. This level requires local operations staff to be in a full readiness state and prepared to move to Tier 2 at a moment’s notice. All personnel need to be trained and aware of emergency planning.

### Tier Two – Implementation of Business Continuity Plan, Preparation for Evacuation.

Tier Two calls for a reduction of staffing levels and an increase in continuity staffs that are to relocate from their normal work sites to alternate locations, establish their ability to conduct operations and prepare to perform essential functions in the event of a catastrophic emergency in preparation for an unmanned facility along with preparation to evacuate from the site.

### Tier Three – Full Evacuation and Remote Operations

Tier Three calls for full evacuation of all personnel onsite and full remote operations. Until it is deemed safe to return to the site.
