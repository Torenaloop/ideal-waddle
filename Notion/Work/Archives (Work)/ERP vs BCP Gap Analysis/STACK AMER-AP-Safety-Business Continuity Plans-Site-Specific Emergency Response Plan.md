# Purpose

1. This STACK Emergency Response Plan (ERP) has been developed to ensure adequate emergency preparedness with rapid and appropriate response to emergency situations. An emergency situation includes but is not limited to the following: anything that threatens employees, contractors, visitors, or the public; disrupts or shuts down operations; or causes property or environmental damage. Emergencies can be natural or man-made, and may involve life-threatening injuries, natural disasters, chemical spills, fires, explosions, and other hazards.
2. **Scope**
    1. This Emergency Response Plan specifically applies to STACK’s operation at <INSERT LOCATION>.
3. **Key Responsibilities**
    2. The Critical Operations Manager is responsible for:
        1. Serving as the incident commander and implementing the emergency response plan as outlined in this document
        2. Assisting with ensuring employees get to assembly point or shelter-in-place location
        3. Coordinating with external emergency services
        4. Ensuring notification and ongoing communications occur with the STACK corporate/senior leadership teams, including following processes outlined with the AMR-AP-Communication-Event Management-Event Management Response procedure
        5. Ensuring employee communications occur during emergency situations, as needed
        6. Ensuring employees know and understand the site emergency response procedures
        7. Supporting and encouraging employee participation in the implementation of the site Emergency Preparedness and Response Plan including emergency drills
    3. Site Security is responsible for:
        8. Being familiar with the Site Emergency Response Plan
        9. Provide support to the Critical Operations Manager during emergency response
        10. If required, Site Security will function as the incident commander until such time as a Critical Operations Manager or Critical Operations Technician assumes the role of incident commander
        11. Facilitate mass communication as required
        12. Assist in the coordination and implementation of emergency drills
    4. The Environmental, Health, and Safety (EHS) team is responsible for:
        13. Facilitating and supporting the development and maintenance of STACK’s Emergency Response Plans at each site
        14. Assisting the Critical Operations Manager in the implementation of this procedure
        15. Providing expertise in emergency response planning, development and roll out
        16. Advising on the applicability and creation of additional hazard-specific emergency response plans (e.g. Spill Prevention, Control and Countermeasure plans (SPCC))
    5. All employees are responsible for:
        17. Being familiar with the Site Emergency Response Plan
        18. Following the emergency procedures outlined in the plan
        19. Following the instructions of the Critical Operation Manager or designated person in charge of the site
        20. Participating in emergency drills
        21. Proceeding to assembly point upon evacuating the building and remaining until dismissed by Management
    6. Oversight of any guests, visitors, or contractors under their supervision during an emergency to ensure their safe evacuation

# Training

11. All employees will receive the following training on the emergency response plan for their assigned site.
    1. Training will be provided at the time of hire and initial implementation of this program. Subsequent training will be provided via annual emergency drills and internal communications.
    2. The training will ensure employees know how to activate the emergency response plans to obtain assistance, evacuate the building and when/where to shelter-in-place.
    3. Employees identified/designated to fulfill a key role in the emergency response plan will receive initial training and subsequent training as the plan changes.

# Emergency Response Procedures

13. Medical Emergencies (911)
    1. In the event of a medical emergency or life-threatening situation, call 911, immediately.
    2. Trained first aid responders should be notified and if willing respond to the situation. Resources identified in section 5c of this plan should be leveraged in the response.
    3. Serious and life-threatening situations are classified as a Sev2 Event and the appropriate processes should be implemented in accordance with AMR-AP-Communication-Event Management-Event Management Response.
    4. Non-emergency medical situations should be brought to the attention of your Critical Operations Manager (COM). If needed, COM will evaluate and ensure the employee receives medical attention.
    5. All incidents should be reported to EHS via the SOSPES tool.
    6. Refer to posted information regarding the location of the nearest Occupational Medical facility for employee treatment of non-911 work-related injuries. Links to the designated clinic(s) and ERs for your site are found on OneSource, under the safety tab.
    7. Notify building security and the COM of all 911 notifications.
    8. Send someone outside to meet emergency responders and escort them to the location of the emergency.
14. Fires CUSTOMIZE THIS SECTION ACCORDINGLY FOR YOUR SITE
    9. This facility is equipped with automatic sprinkler systems, VESDA, and fire alarms.
    10. Fire alarms are located throughout the facility and can be used to indicate a fire and the need for evacuation. All people should evacuate the building when and alarm sounds.
    11. Proceed to the nearest exit and go to the designated assembly area.
    12. Fire extinguishers are mounted throughout the facility. If trained to their use, they may be used to extinguish a small fire (e.g. roughly 3’x 3’ in size or approximately the size of a small trash can). If the fire is larger than what can be extinguished with a single extinguisher, attempts to extinguish the fire should end, personnel should evacuate and wait for professional emergency services.
    13. Fire emergencies are classified as a Sev1 Event and the appropriate processes should be implemented in accordance with AMR-AP-Communication-Event Management-Event Management Response.
    14. Critical Operations Team should ensure that client representatives are made aware of the emergency.
    15. Critical Operations Manager or their designee should:
        1. Assume the role of incident commander and obtain a headcount of those that have evacuated.
        2. Communicate with local emergency responders.
        3. Notify STACK Operational Leadership, EHS, and Client Success about the emergency situation.
        4. Follow all media relations protocols in place from STACK Marketing.
15. Diesel Fuel Spill or Release
    16. Upon discovery of a diesel fuel release/spill, notify Critical Operations Manager immediately.
    17. Remain clear of the release/spill unless you are trained and authorized to assist with the spill response.
    18. Follow the spill response procedures outlined within the site’s SPCC plan.
    19. Refer to section 5c of this plan for the location of spill kits.
    20. Notify STACK EHS about the emergency situation. They will provide assistance and support for any needed regulatory notifications.
16. Chemical Spill or Release
    21. Upon discovery of a spill or release from oil storage infrastructure, notify Critical Operations Manager immediately. <Insert reference to existing EOPs>
    22. Remain clear of the spill/release unless you are trained and authorized to assist with spill response activities.
    23. Refer to the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the chemical that has been released. Identify proper PPE and spill response measures on the SDS.
    24. Contain the spill in a safe and controlled manner. Leverage resources identified in section 5c of this plan.
    25. Notify STACK EHS about the emergency situation and they will provide further guidance on regulatory reporting requirements.
17. Active Shooter
    26. If safe to do so, call 911 and/or notify STACK Security.
    27. The priority should be to determine the most reasonable way to protect your own life.
    28. Follow the simple principle of RUN – HIDE - FIGHT
        5. Run: Identify an escape route, leaving all belongings behind, run to create distance between yourself and the threat.
        6. Hide: If you can’t escape, hide in an area out of the active shooter’s view. If possible secure yourself in a locked area behind doors without windows. Block entry to your hiding place, silence radios and cell phones, stay quiet and be prepared to adjust to the situation as necessary.
        7. Fight: As a last resort, and only when your life is in imminent danger should you attempt to incapacitate the active shooter. Act with physical aggression, utilize nearby objects as weapons, attempt to create space and get away from the situation.
18. Earthquake
    29. Follow the simple principle of DROP, COVER, HOLD ON
    30. Get under something solid like a desk or table
    31. Stay away from windows, glass walls and skylights
    32. Be prepared for aftershocks
    33. Stay in the building unless you are in immediate danger
    34. Do not use elevators
    35. If it is necessary to evacuate, go to an open space away from buildings to avoid falling window glass and debris
    36. Following the earthquake, the Critical Operations Manager (COM) should assume the role of Incident Commander and convene the Critical Operation Team to:
        8. Determine if the severity of the earthquake warrants evacuating the building, if so, initiate evacuation and follow evacuation procedures
        9. Evaluate status of employees and check for injuries among co-workers
        10. Provide first aid, if willing and able
        11. If evacuation was necessary, the COM should obtain a headcount of all employees, including names and contact numbers
        12. Instruct employees not to leave the site without “checking out” by notifying the COM of their intended departure
        13. Notify STACK EHS and provide status update
19. Severe Weather, Tropical Storms, and Flooding
    37. Stay tuned to weather reports, as many severe weather events can be forecasted hours before they occur, and early planning can ensure safety
    38. Proceed to the designated shelter-in-place location to seek cover and protection.
    39. If flooding is occurring move to second floor of building, if possible
    40. Evacuations – if an evacuation is appropriate, communicate with management that you and co-workers are leaving the site and will be going home. Do not leave the site without notifying your management
    41. Stay away from damaged areas and downed power lines
    42. Following the severe weather event, the Critical Operations Manager (COM) should assume the role of Incident Commander and convene the Critical Operations Team to:
        14. Evaluate status of employees and check for injuries among co-workers
        15. Provide first aid, if willing and able
        16. Obtain a headcount of all employees, including names and contact numbers
        17. Instruct employees not to leave the site without “checking out” by notifying the COM of their intended departure
        18. Notify STACK EHS and provide status update
20. Tornados, Cyclones and Hurricanes
    43. Stay tuned to weather reports, as many severe weather events can be forecasted hours before they occur, and early planning can ensure safety
    44. Proceed to the designated shelter-in-place location and seek cover and protection within the facility.
    45. Remain in place – Once tornado, cyclone or hurricane has passed, do not immediately evacuate unless it is safe to do so.
    46. Do not leave the site without notifying your management
    47. Most injuries occur from falling glass, plaster, debris, and electrical lines as people are leaving damaged buildings; use caution and assume all electrical lines are energized
    48. Stay away from damaged areas and downed power lines
    49. Following the event, the Critical Operations Manager (COM) should assume the role of Incident Commander and convene the Critical Operations Team to:
        19. Evaluate status of employees and check for injuries among co-workers
        20. Provide first aid, if willing and able
        21. Obtain a headcount of all employees, including names and contact numbers
        22. Instruct employees not to leave the site without “checking out” by notifying the COM of their intended departure
        23. Evaluate facility and surroundings to identify immediate hazards, inform employees of the hazards and keep employees away from hazardous areas
        24. Notify STACK EHS and provide status update
