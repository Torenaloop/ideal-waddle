[The ](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[**VARK model of learning**](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[ is a popular concept in psychology and education that identifies four main types of learners: visual, auditory, reading/writing, and kinesthetic ](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[1](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156). The idea is that students learn best when teaching methods and school activities match their learning styles, strengths, and preferences. [However, most evidence suggests that personal learning preferences have little to no actual influence on learning outcomes ](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[1](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156).

[Neil Fleming introduced an inventory in 1987 that was designed to help students and others learn more about their individual learning preferences ](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[1](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156). According to the VARK model, learners are identified by whether they have a preference for:

- Visual learning (pictures, movies, diagrams)
- Auditory learning (music, discussion, lectures)
- Reading and writing (making lists, reading textbooks, taking notes)
- Kinesthetic learning (movement, experiments, hands-on activities)

The VARK model refers to the four sensory modalities that describe different learning preferences. [The model suggests that these modalities reflect how students learn best ](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156)[1](https://www.verywellmind.com/vark-learning-styles-2795156).
